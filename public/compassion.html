What This Pillar Is

Love With Its Sleeves Rolled Up

A Gospel that feeds the soul and ignores the body has misread its own founder. Christ healed, fed, touched the untouchable and defended the defenceless — and He did it for people who never followed Him afterwards.

This pillar is that instinct, organised. Eight arms covering child protection, rescue, medical aid, social security, feeding, education support and community health. All of it offered without conditions: nobody is asked to attend a service, sign anything, or profess anything to receive help.

“Pure and undefiled religion before God and the Father is this: to visit orphans and widows in their trouble, and to keep oneself unspotted from the world.” James 1:27

Four commitments that govern everything here

  • No conditions — help is never traded for attendance, conversion or loyalty
  • Dignity first — people are participants in their own recovery, not recipients of our charity
  • Statutory first, always — where the law provides a protection service, we work with it, not around it
  • Aid is a bridge — the goal is a person who no longer needs us, not a permanent caseload

That last point matters most. Relief that never becomes rehabilitation, and rehabilitation that never becomes independence, produces dependency dressed as compassion. Every arm below has an exit built into it.

CSPA & CYRR
Arms 01 & 05

Children & Rescue

CSPA keeps children safe inside our own institutions and advocates for those at risk outside them. CYRR works with children and young people already on the street or in crisis.

This is the most tightly governed work we do. Every worker is screened and supervised, statutory authorities are involved from the first day of any case, and no child is ever placed in an unregulated setting.

Policy & Training

A single safeguarding policy applied across every congregation, network and field site

Referral & Escalation

A named safeguarding lead in every location, with same-day escalation to statutory services

Street Outreach

Consistent, patient contact work with children living on the street — the same faces, week after week

Transitional Care

Short-term supported accommodation in registered facilities while a longer-term plan is built

Family Reunification

Wherever it is safe, the aim is a child restored to family — assessed properly, never rushed

Arms 03 & 08

Health & Medical

MIA funds urgent treatment for people who would otherwise go without it. HACO runs the preventive work — mobile clinics, screening and health education in underserved communities.

Both are staffed by qualified clinicians and operate strictly within their scope of practice. We do not offer medical advice or treatment through untrained volunteers, and we never present prayer as a substitute for care.

Treatment Grants

Assessed funding toward surgery, medication and hospital costs, paid directly to the provider

Mobile Clinics

Regular clinic days in communities with poor access, run alongside city mission campaigns

Screening & Prevention

Blood pressure, diabetes, maternal and child health screening with proper onward referral

Immunisation Support

Logistics, venue and volunteer support for national immunisation campaigns

Mental Health Referral

Trained listeners who provide support and refer onward — never a substitute for clinical care

Arms 02 & 04

Social Security

CVSW carries the quiet, unglamorous work: visiting widows, seniors and the chronically ill who are otherwise alone. SSF is the financial safety net behind it.

In most of the places we work, there is no functioning state welfare system. SSF is a deliberate attempt to build one at community scale — funded by members, governed transparently, and open to neighbours who are not members at all.

Home Visitation

Regular visits by trained, paired volunteers — the same people each time, so trust can build

Emergency Grants

Rapid, small, assessed grants for rent, food, funeral costs or a sudden loss of income

Savings Groups

Member-run savings and lending circles, run with CIEL, that build resilience before crisis hits

Benefits & Rights Advocacy

Help navigating whatever statutory entitlements do exist — forms, appeals and representation

Widow Property Protection

Legal support for widows facing dispossession, working with the Law & Justice sphere

Arms 06 & 07

Relief & Education

AFER handles food and emergency response — feeding programmes, disaster relief and support for displaced families. CES keeps children in school when money is the only reason they would leave.

These two are paired deliberately. Hunger and interrupted schooling are the two fastest routes from a difficult childhood to a permanently limited adulthood, and both are cheap to prevent compared with the cost of the outcome.

Feeding Programmes

Regular community kitchens and school feeding, run by local volunteers with local suppliers

Disaster Response

Rapid deployment of food, water, bedding and shelter after flooding, fire or displacement

School Fees & Materials

Fees, uniforms and books paid directly to schools, with attendance reviewed each term

Tutoring & Catch-Up

After-school support for children who have lost ground, delivered by trained volunteers

Vocational Placement

For young people past school age: trade training and apprenticeship placement through CIEL

Method

Relief, Rehabilitation, Development

Most harm done by well-meaning charity comes from applying relief to a problem that needed development. These are three different responses, and confusing them is expensive.

Stage 01 · Relief

Stop the Bleeding

Immediate, unconditional and short. Food, shelter, emergency cash, urgent treatment. Appropriate only in genuine crisis, and deliberately time-limited — relief extended indefinitely becomes dependency.

Stage 02 · Rehabilitation

Restore What Was Lost

Rebuilding the person's own capacity: returning a child to school, treating a chronic condition, restoring a family, repairing a livelihood. The person does the work; we resource it.

Stage 03 · Development

Build Beyond the Crisis

Skills, savings, enterprise and employment through CIEL. The point at which a household stops being vulnerable to the next shock rather than merely surviving the last one.

Stage 04 · Exit

Close the Case Well

A planned, agreed exit with a follow-up check at six and twelve months. Cases that never close are reviewed — usually the sign of a wrongly diagnosed need.

What We Will Not Do

  • Require attendance, conversion or profession of faith for help
  • Photograph or name a beneficiary without informed consent
  • Place a child in an unregistered or unregulated facility
  • Bypass statutory child protection or medical services
  • Offer clinical treatment or advice through untrained volunteers
  • Present prayer as a replacement for medical care
  • Give cash directly where paying a provider is safer for the person

If you have experienced any of the above from anyone acting in our name, please tell us using the form below. We will investigate.

Safeguarding & Accountability

Ministries that work with vulnerable people attract, among other things, people who wish to exploit vulnerable people. Pretending otherwise is how harm happens.

Screening

Background checks, references and interview for every worker and volunteer with any access to children or vulnerable adults.

Annual Training

Mandatory safeguarding training every year, refreshed and recorded. Lapsed training removes you from the rota automatically.

Never Alone

Two-adult rule in every setting, visible spaces, no unsupervised private contact, and no personal messaging with a minor.

Same-Day Escalation

Any concern goes to the safeguarding lead the same day and to statutory services where the threshold is met. No internal delay.

Accountability, including for us

The temptation in every organisation is to handle allegations quietly to protect its reputation. That instinct is precisely what allows abuse to continue, and we treat it as a disqualifying failure of leadership rather than a regrettable judgement call.

  • Allegations against staff or leaders are handled by the same process as anyone else — seniority is not a mitigating factor
  • Statutory authorities are informed where the threshold is met, regardless of the consequences to us
  • No confidentiality agreement is ever used to silence a complainant
  • An annual safeguarding report goes to the apostolic council and is available on request
  • Finances for every arm are independently reviewed and published annually

Financial Transparency

  • Every arm publishes annual income and expenditure
  • Independent review of all compassion accounts
  • Donors receive a report on what their giving funded
  • Administrative costs disclosed as a percentage, not hidden
  • No arm accepts funds with conditions attached to beneficiaries

Questions

Frequently Asked

Do I have to be a Christian to receive help?

No. Eligibility is based on need alone. Nobody is asked about their faith, invited to a service as a condition, or treated differently for declining. Attaching conditions to charity turns it into a transaction.

How do I apply for assistance?

Contact the compassion office or speak to any local congregation, chapter or field site. Each arm has published referral criteria, and an assessor will contact you. Urgent medical and child protection cases are triaged the same day.

Why do you pay providers directly instead of giving cash?

For treatment and school fees, paying the provider protects the person from pressure to divert the money and gives us a verifiable record. For emergency relief we do give cash directly, because in a crisis people know their own needs better than we do.

What is your position on prayer and medical treatment?

We pray with anyone who wants prayer, and we never present it as an alternative to medical care. No worker of ours is permitted to discourage someone from taking medication, attending an appointment or following clinical advice. That is a dismissible offence.

Do you run children's homes or orphanages?

We do not operate residential institutions for children. Evidence overwhelmingly favours family-based care, so CYRR works toward reunification or registered foster placement, using short-term transitional care in registered facilities only where necessary.

Can I visit a project or bring a team?

Visits are possible with notice, and we welcome skilled teams. We do not host trips where the primary purpose is the visitors' experience, and we do not permit photography of beneficiaries, particularly children, without informed consent.

How much of my giving reaches the field?

Administrative costs are disclosed as a percentage in the annual report rather than buried. Volunteers are unpaid, most field logistics are donated, and the report shows exactly what proportion of each arm's spend was programme delivery.
