What This Pillar Is

Nations Are Discipled, Not Just Evangelised

The commission was to disciple nations — not merely to gather individuals out of them. A nation is discipled when its institutions become honest, its courts become reachable by the poor, its schools form character, and its citizens stop treating public property as nobody's property.

That work is slow, unglamorous and almost entirely invisible in its early decades. It is also the difference between a country where the Gospel is preached freely and one where it is preached into rubble.

“Righteousness exalts a nation, but sin is a reproach to any people.” Proverbs 14:34

The diagnosis we work from

Most national dysfunction is not a resource problem. Nations with abundant resources routinely fail while nations with almost none succeed. The difference is nearly always institutional: whether contracts are enforced, whether office is a trust or a harvest, whether a citizen believes the rules apply to everyone.

  • Character precedes capacity — competent people without integrity accelerate decline rather than reversing it
  • Institutions over personalities — a good ruler with bad institutions leaves nothing behind
  • Citizenship, not spectatorship — a nation gets the civic culture its ordinary people practise daily
  • Truth before reconciliation — grievances buried unaddressed reappear as violence a generation later
  • Generational patience — we plan in decades, and refuse to promise transformation inside an election cycle

Read Our Posture

Build, Don't Lament

Complaint is cheap and abundant. This pillar trades exclusively in things that can be built, staffed, funded and handed over.

Non-Partisan by Constitution

We endorse no party and no candidate, in any nation, ever. Principles are stated publicly; political choices are left to citizens.

Handed Over, Not Held

Every institution we start has a written handover plan to indigenous leadership from the day it is founded.

5 Workstreams
The Flagship

Rebuild-Nigeria

A twenty-year national reconstruction initiative, run by Nigerians, for Nigeria — and designed from the beginning to be copied. The template is deliberately portable: any nation can run Rebuild-[Nation] using the same five workstreams.

RN is not an advocacy campaign. Each workstream owns a concrete deliverable, publishes an annual report, and is judged on what actually exists at the end of the year.

01 — People

Training and placing people of proven integrity into public service, the judiciary and civic institutions

02 — Institutions

Practical reform work with agencies willing to improve procurement, records and accountability

03 — Civic Culture

Reorientation work in schools, congregations and communities on citizenship and public honesty

04 — Peace & Security

Community mediation, early-warning networks and reconciliation in conflict-affected areas

05 — Livelihoods

Enterprise, skills and land productivity through CIEL, because poverty is the soil corruption grows in

Structure

The Bodies of This Pillar

Five specialist bodies alongside Rebuild-Nigeria, each with a defined mandate, its own faculty and its own reporting line.

Body 01Integrity

Centre for Patriotism & Integrity in Governance

CPIG works on the single variable that most determines whether a nation functions: whether the people holding public office treat it as a trust or a harvest. It does this through formation, standards and practical institutional reform — not through denunciation.

The centre works with any agency willing to improve, regardless of who leads it, and publishes its methodology openly so that others can use it without needing us.

What CPIG does

  • An integrity formation programme for serving public officers
  • Practical reform engagements: procurement, record-keeping, complaint handling
  • A published code of conduct for believers in public office
  • Research on the mechanics of corruption in specific sectors
  • Support and peer accountability for officers under pressure to comply

Body 02Training

Institute of Governance & Strategic Leadership

IGSL is the training arm: a serious school of public administration for people entering or already serving in government, the civil service, the judiciary and local administration.

The curriculum is technical as well as ethical. Good intentions with no grasp of budgeting, procurement law or policy design produce honest officials who are nonetheless ineffective — and ineffectiveness is its own kind of betrayal.

Core curriculum

  • Constitutional order, separation of powers and the rule of law
  • Public finance: budgeting, procurement, audit and oversight
  • Policy design, implementation and honest evaluation
  • Ethics under pressure: bribery, patronage, nepotism, whistleblowing
  • Strategic leadership, negotiation and institutional change
  • Practicum: a placement with a serving officer or agency

Body 03Peace

Institute for Peace, Security & Development

IPSD works in and around conflict: community mediation, early-warning networks, and the long work of rebuilding trust between groups who have hurt each other.

Its operating assumption is that most local conflict has material causes — land, water, grazing routes, unemployment — which are then given religious or ethnic language. Addressing only the language leaves the cause intact.

What IPSD does

  • Trained community mediators embedded in conflict-affected areas
  • Early-warning networks that surface tension before it becomes violence
  • Interfaith and inter-community dialogue convened on neutral ground
  • Trauma care and reintegration support for displaced families
  • Practical work on the underlying resource disputes, with CIEL
  • Research and briefings for security and civil authorities

Body 04Citizenship

Centre for National Reorientation & Citizenship

A nation's civic culture is the sum of what ordinary people do when nobody is checking: whether they queue, pay, litter, cheat an exam, or hand over a bribe to save an afternoon. CNRC works on exactly that layer.

Reorientation campaigns have a poor reputation because they are usually slogans. CNRC works instead through curriculum, sustained community programmes and measurable behaviour change over years.

Programmes

  • A citizenship curriculum for schools and congregations
  • Community campaigns on public property, queuing, waste and honesty
  • Civic education on rights, entitlements and how to use them
  • Voter education — how to evaluate any candidate, never whom to choose
  • Public honesty pledges with actual follow-up, not one-day events
  • Pre- and post-programme surveys published in full

Body 05Justice

Truth & Justice Commission

TJC exists because reconciliation without truth is only a truce. It documents unresolved grievances, supports victims in accessing legal remedy, and convenes truth-telling processes where communities are willing.

It is not a court and claims no judicial power. Its work is documentation, advocacy, legal support and the patient creation of conditions in which acknowledgement becomes possible.

What TJC does

  • Documentation of unresolved grievances and communal harm
  • Legal aid and representation for people unable to afford it
  • Convened truth-telling processes, entered voluntarily by all parties
  • Advocacy on access to justice, court delay and legal aid provision
  • Support for victims through statutory processes
  • Work with the Law & Justice sphere on systemic reform

Commitments

The Citizen's Charter

Seven commitments we ask of anyone associated with this pillar — and hold ourselves to first. They describe private conduct, because that is where public integrity is actually decided.

I — I will not give or take a bribe

Including where it is normal, expected, small, or costs me time and money to refuse. This is the first article because it is the one most often waived quietly, and because a movement that concedes it has nothing left to say about governance.

II — I will treat public property as a public trust

Public funds, vehicles, offices, time and materials are not unowned. I will not convert them to private use, and I will not treat access to them as a reward for loyalty.

III — I will appoint and promote on merit

Family, tribe, faith and friendship are not qualifications. Where I hold power to hire, I will use it to find the most competent person of good character — including when that costs me a relationship.

IV — I will tell the truth in public matters

In records, reports, testimony and accounts. I will not sign what I know to be false, inflate what I know to be modest, or stay silent where silence functions as a lie.

V — I will not defend wrongdoing because it is ours

Wrong done by my party, my church, my tribe or my friend is still wrong. I will hold my own side to the standard I demand of others, and I will accept the cost of doing so.

VI — I will protect the vulnerable in my reach

Children, the poor, the elderly, the stranger and anyone who cannot answer back. Where I see harm, I will act or report rather than look away because it is inconvenient.

VII — I will build something that outlasts me

And hand it over willingly. I will train a successor, document what I know, and measure my service by what still functions after I leave rather than by what depends on me staying.

Be Clear

Where We Stand Politically

Religious bodies working near government have a long record of becoming instruments of whoever is currently in power. We would rather state our limits publicly than be asked about them later.

Holy Nation Global endorses no political party and no candidate

In any nation, at any level, in any election. This is a standing policy and is not subject to circumstance, invitation or funding.

What we will do

  • Train and support citizens of integrity to enter and serve in public office
  • State principles publicly — honesty, justice, protection of the vulnerable — and apply them consistently
  • Work with any government, agency or officer willing to improve, regardless of party
  • Publish research, methodology and evidence openly for anyone to use
  • Educate citizens on how to evaluate candidates, and leave the choice entirely to them
  • Say plainly when something is wrong, including when the wrong is done by people who support us

What we will not do

  • Endorse a party, candidate or ticket, or permit our platforms to be used to do so
  • Accept funding conditioned on political support or silence
  • Deliver a political message from the pulpit or through our networks
  • Treat a political outcome as the arrival of the Kingdom, or a defeat as its loss
  • Provide religious cover for any government's conduct
  • Withhold criticism of an ally, or manufacture criticism of an opponent

The Pipeline

From Citizen to Public Servant

How a person moves from wanting the country to change to actually being able to change part of it.

Stage 01

Formation

The Kingdom Institute and the Citizen's Charter. Character and conviction come first, because everything downstream depends on a person who will not sell.

Stage 02

Technical Training

IGSL: public finance, policy design, constitutional order, ethics under pressure. Competence is not optional and cannot be improvised on arrival.

Stage 03

Placement

A supervised placement inside a working agency, civil service department or civic institution. Reality tests the classroom.

Stage 04

Service & Support

Entering office with a peer group, a mentor and a confidential line for when the pressure to compromise arrives — because it will.

Stage 05

Succession

Train a replacement, document the work, hand over cleanly. An officer who cannot be replaced has not built anything.

Support in Office

  • A confidential peer group of officers facing the same pressures
  • An assigned mentor outside your chain of command
  • Legal advice where refusing to comply carries risk
  • Practical support if standing firm costs you the post
  • No expectation of favours, contracts or access in return

We do not ask officers we have trained to do anything for us. If we did, everything else on this page would be theatre.

Questions

Frequently Asked

Are you a political organisation?

No. We train citizens, work with institutions and state principles. We endorse no party or candidate anywhere, we do not campaign, and we do not permit our platforms to be used for political messaging. See "Where We Stand" above for the full policy.

Do I have to be a Christian to train at IGSL?

No. IGSL is open to people of all faiths and none. The ethics curriculum is grounded in our convictions and taught transparently as such, but nobody is required to share them to enrol, complete or be certified.

Why focus on Nigeria first?

Because it is where the movement began, where most of our people live, and because a model should be proven somewhere before it is exported. The template is deliberately portable and several nations are already adapting it.

Isn't this just religion getting into politics?

Citizens with religious convictions participating in public life is ordinary democratic life, not a takeover. What would be objectionable is a religious body directing votes, buying influence or claiming divine sanction for a party — and all three are things we have explicitly ruled out.

What if someone you trained becomes corrupt?

We say so. Training and association are withdrawn, and we do not offer character references, public defence or religious cover. Our credibility depends entirely on being harder on our own than on anyone else.

Does TJC have legal authority?

None. It is not a court and cannot summon, compel, try or sanction anyone. Its work is documentation, legal aid, advocacy and voluntary truth-telling processes that all parties enter freely.

Can my country run Rebuild?

Yes. The five-workstream template, curricula, reporting tools and evaluation methodology are free to use and adapt. We ask only that you keep the annual public reporting, including reporting what failed.
