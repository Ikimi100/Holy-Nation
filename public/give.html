First Things

Why We Give — and How We Refuse To Ask

Money is the subject on which the Church has most often lost the trust of ordinary people, and usually not because it asked, but because of how it asked. So before any account numbers appear on this page, here is our position.

“So let each one give as he purposes in his heart, not grudgingly or of necessity; for God loves a cheerful giver.” 2 Corinthians 9:7

What giving is

Giving is worship and stewardship. It is an act of trust that says what we hold is not ultimately ours, and a practical act of love that funds work we could not do alone. The tithe — a tenth — is the historic baseline of that discipline. The offering is what a person purposes beyond it. First fruits mark the beginning of a season by giving before spending.

What giving is not

It is not a transaction with God, a payment for blessing, or a lever that moves His hand. We do not teach seed-faith arithmetic, we do not promise returns, and we do not tell anyone that a gift will heal them, enrich them or fix a crisis. Anyone teaching that in our name is misrepresenting this house.

  • Giving is never a condition of belonging. Nobody is asked to give to attend, join, be counted, or be cared for
  • Giving buys nothing. Not access, not position, not influence, not a place on any council
  • Amounts are never published or ranked. No giving boards, no donor tiers read aloud, no name on a wall
  • Nobody is pursued. We do not chase lapsed givers or telephone people about money
  • You will never be asked to give as a visitor. This is stated on every page of this site

Please Do Not Give If…

  • You need the money for rent, food, medicine or school fees
  • You would have to borrow, or delay a debt repayment, to do it
  • You are being pressured by anyone, including by a leader
  • You are giving in the hope of a specific outcome in return
  • You are in a season of financial crisis — let us help you instead

If any of these is true, the Compassion pillar exists for exactly that. Ask for help without embarrassment; it is offered on need alone, with no faith test and no expectation of anything back.

A Fee, Not a Gift
Global College

Tuition & Fees

College tuition is a payment for a service, not a donation — and we keep the two completely separate in the accounts, the receipts and the language we use about them.

Tuition covers materials, assessment and administration only. Fees are set regionally so they stay proportionate to local income, and are confirmed in writing at the point of admission.

How It's Governed
Non-Negotiable

Financial Accountability

Movements go wrong with money in predictable ways. Each control below exists because of a specific failure we have watched happen elsewhere.

Two-Person Rule

No single person ever handles, counts, banks or authorises funds alone. Cash is counted by two unrelated people who both sign.

Independent Review

Accounts are reviewed annually by an external firm with no other relationship to the movement and no member on our councils.

Separated Duties

The people who receive money are not the people who authorise spending, and neither group reconciles the accounts.

Published Annually

Income and expenditure by fund, administrative cost as a percentage, and every region's report — available to anyone on request.

Rules that apply to leadership too

The most common failure is not theft. It is a founder whose personal finances and the ministry's finances quietly become the same thing, with nobody positioned to ask about it.

  • No leader sets their own pay. Stipends are set by a committee that excludes the person being paid
  • No personal use of ministry funds or assets. No exceptions for seniority, and no loans to leaders
  • Related-party transactions are declared and approved before they happen, not disclosed afterwards
  • Every leader's expenses are reviewed by someone who does not report to them
  • Any council member may call an audit without needing the presiding apostle's agreement
  • Financial misconduct is handled as misconduct — seniority is not a mitigating factor

What we will not do

  • Accept funds conditioned on a decision, an appointment or our silence
  • Sell access, position, prayer, prophecy, anointing oil or any spiritual benefit
  • Rank, publish or read out what individuals have given
  • Pressure anyone from a platform, or use emotional appeals about crisis to raise money
  • Take a gift from someone we have reason to believe cannot afford it
  • Use a beneficiary's story or photograph to raise funds without their informed consent

Raise a Financial Concern

  • Any concern about funds goes to the safeguarding line
  • Concerns about leaders follow the same route as any other
  • Anonymous reports are accepted and still investigated
  • No confidentiality agreement is ever used to silence a complainant
  • You will be told the outcome, unless the law prevents it

If something about how money is handled here does not look right to you, please say so. We would far rather answer an awkward question than lose your trust quietly.

Report a Concern
Paperwork

Receipts & Tax

Every gift is receipted if you want one, and an annual giving statement is issued on request in January for the previous year. Tuition payments receive a separate invoice and receipt, because they are a fee rather than a gift.

Tax relief varies by country

Whether your giving attracts tax relief depends on where you live and on our registration status in that country. We will tell you plainly what applies in your case rather than implying a benefit that may not exist.

  • United Kingdom — Gift Aid where we hold the relevant registration and you have made a declaration
  • United States — deductibility depends on the receiving entity's status; ask before assuming
  • Canada — official receipts only where a registered charity is the recipient
  • Nigeria and elsewhere — treatment varies; the finance office will confirm in writing

We are not tax advisers and will not pretend to be. For anything beyond a receipt, please speak to a qualified adviser in your own country.

Your Data

  • We never see or store your card details — the processor handles them
  • Your giving record is visible only to the finance officer
  • We do not sell, rent or share donor data with anyone, ever
  • You can give anonymously; the fund is recorded, you are not
  • You can ask us to delete your record at any time
  • No mailing list is added without you asking for it

Anonymous giving means we genuinely cannot issue a tax receipt for it. That is the trade-off, and it is yours to make.

Fraud & Impersonation

People impersonate churches to steal from their members. It happens to us, and it will happen to you if you are not told what to expect.

Nobody from Holy Nation will ever direct-message you asking for money

Not the presiding apostle, not your coordinator, not a leader you know by name. Not by WhatsApp, SMS, email, Facebook or any other channel. If it happens, it is a scam — no matter how convincing the profile picture is.

We will never

  • Message you privately asking for a transfer, gift card or mobile money top-up
  • Ask for your bank login, card PIN, OTP or one-time code — for any reason
  • Give account details that differ from the ones published on this page
  • Ask you to keep a payment request confidential from other leaders
  • Create urgency — "before midnight", "only ten slots", "the anointing lifts at…"
  • Offer you a job, a visa or a placement abroad in exchange for a fee

If you are contacted

  • Do not pay. Nothing is lost by waiting to verify
  • Screenshot it — the profile, the number and the message
  • Send it to theholynationglobal@gmail.com so we can warn the community
  • Report the account to the platform, and to police if money was taken
  • Tell your group. The same scammer usually works through a whole list

If you have already sent money, please tell us anyway. Nobody here will make you feel foolish, and knowing helps us protect the next person.

Questions

Frequently Asked

Do I have to tithe to belong here?

No. Giving is never a condition of membership, attendance, care, training or anything else. Nobody checks, nobody asks, and the register records funds rather than what individuals gave. We teach the tithe as a discipline, not as a debt we collect.

Should my tithe go to you or to my local church?

To your local church. If you belong to a congregation that feeds and cares for you, that is where the tithe belongs — and most of our members belong to churches with no relationship to us at all. Give to us out of what you purpose beyond that.

Where does my money physically go — to Lagos?

No. Giving stays in the region it was given in and is governed by that region's council. Lagos does not sweep national accounts. The only funds that move between regions are voluntary contributions from established regions to early-stage ones, and those carry no conditions.

Does giving more get me influence or a position?

No, and this is structural rather than aspirational. Each region has one voice in the commonwealth regardless of contribution, appointments are on merit and character, and funders who attempt to attach conditions are refused. Kingdom Builder partnership gives you briefings and reports, not votes.

Can I give anonymously?

Yes. The fund is recorded; you are not. The trade-off is that we cannot then issue a tax receipt or a giving statement, because there is nothing to attach them to.

Can I stop or change a recurring gift?

At any time, without giving a reason, and nobody will contact you to ask why. When card processing is live you will be able to do it yourself from the receipt email. For standing orders, your bank cancels it directly — you do not need our permission.

What happens if a fund raises more than it needs?

We write to the people who gave to it and ask before moving anything. Restricted funds cannot be quietly redirected to cover a shortfall elsewhere, and that restriction is held in the accounts, not just in our intentions.

Is tuition a donation?

No, and we keep them separate deliberately. Tuition is a fee paid for a service, invoiced and receipted as such. Calling fees "gifts" muddles the accounts and, in some countries, the tax position. Bursaries paid from the Scholarship Fund are the gift; the tuition itself is not.

Someone messaged me asking for money in your name. Is that you?

No. Nobody from this movement will ever direct-message you asking for a payment, a transfer, bank details or a one-time code. Screenshot it and send it to theholynationglobal@gmail.com so we can warn everyone else.

Can I see the accounts?

Yes. Income and expenditure by fund, administrative cost as a percentage, and the independent reviewer's statement are published annually and available to anyone who asks — member or not, giver or not.
