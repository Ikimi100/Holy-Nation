Who We Are

A Small House at the Edge of the Map

Holy Nation Australia began among African and Filipino families in western Sydney and has grown into a genuinely mixed community — migrants, second-generation Australians, and people whose families have been here for generations.

We are small, and we are spread across a continent roughly the size of Europe with a population smaller than Kenya's. Almost everything about how this community works is shaped by those two facts.

“From the rising of the sun to its going down, the LORD's name is to be praised.” Psalm 113:3

Acknowledgement of Country

We acknowledge the Traditional Owners of the lands on which our groups meet, and pay our respects to Elders past and present. Where our members engage with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities, that engagement is led by First Nations people themselves — we follow, and we do not run programmes of our own design.

What we are, and what we are not

  • You keep your church. Most of our members belong to an Australian congregation already
  • You get known. Every member is placed in a city group or the online fellowship
  • You get trained. Global College cohorts run on Australian evenings
  • You carry something. Australia holds Watch 04, the first watch of the global day
  • You are never asked for money to belong. Ever

Our place in the movement

Oceania is listed as "under prayer" in the ThyKingdomCome roadmap — no constituted regional council, no staff, no timeline. Australia is a national community, not a region, and we describe ourselves that way deliberately. We relate directly to the global commonwealth rather than through a regional structure that does not yet exist.

Watch 04

The Watch That Opens the Day

A quirk of geography turned into an assignment.

Why this matters more than it sounds

Watches run in each region's local time, which is how the global altar stays unbroken across twenty-four hours. Australia holds Watch 04 — The Dawn Watch, 3:00 to 6:00 AM, whose standing burden is breakthrough, new mercies and prophetic release.

Because of where we sit relative to the date line, Australia's dawn watch is the first watch of each new day anywhere in the movement. When our intercessors are praying at 3:00 AM Sydney time, it is still the previous afternoon in Lagos and the previous morning in Houston.

So the day of this global house opens here, every single day, in a small country at the edge of the map with three city groups and no regional council. There is something in that arrangement worth noticing.

  • One three-hour window, one day a week — almost always shared
  • New intercessors are paired with an experienced watchman for two months
  • The Sydney tower gathers physically for this watch monthly
  • Nobody is thought less of for standing down from a 3:00 AM watch

What We Pray First

  • The nations whose day is about to begin
  • The mission teams deploying that day
  • The watches about to open in Asia and Africa
  • Breakthrough in situations that have not moved
  • The Australian church, and our own city

Watch reports from Australia go into the global system before most regions have woken up. What we see is often the first thing the command reads that day.

About the Online Group
Built for It

The Problem of Distance

Perth is further from Sydney than London is from Moscow. A member in regional Queensland may be six hours from the nearest city group. In most churches that means those people quietly drift.

So we built the online group as a real group with a real host and real commitments — not an overflow room for people who could not make the proper meeting.

A Named Host

The online group has its own host who knows every member by name and checks in personally

Same Rhythm

Thursday nights, same teaching, same discussion, same prayer — not a watered-down version

Between Meetings

Members are paired so that somebody notices within a week if you disappear

Once a Year, In Person

Travel support so that distance does not mean never meeting anyone face to face

Watches Work Anywhere

A watch can be held from a farmhouse in the Riverina exactly as well as from Parramatta

Questions

Frequently Asked

Do I have to leave my church to join?

No. Most of our members belong to an Australian congregation already, and we would discourage anyone from leaving. City groups meet midweek and the training strengthens you wherever you serve.

I'm in Perth. Is this worth joining?

Yes. The online group is a proper group with its own host, the same Thursday rhythm and paired members who notice if you go quiet. A third of our members are in it, and travel support means you meet people face to face at least once a year.

What about New Zealand?

We have a handful of New Zealand members who join the online group and the Wednesday cohort. New Zealand is not a separate community and we would not describe it as one. If three of you there want to begin something, write to us.

Why isn't Australia a ThyKingdomCome region?

Because Oceania is listed as "under prayer" — no council, no staff, no timeline. We are a national community and we say so. Marking ourselves as a region would look better and be untrue.

What safeguarding applies here?

Anyone working with children or vulnerable adults holds a current Working With Children Check for their state plus annual training. Two adults present at all times, meetings in visible spaces, no private messaging with minors, and same-day escalation of any concern.

Is there a membership fee?

None. Community, gatherings, watches and the Foundations Class are free. Global College tuition is set regionally with instalment plans and bursaries, and nobody has been turned away for inability to pay.

Are you politically affiliated?

No. Holy Nation Global endorses no party and no candidate in any nation, and our groups do not permit campaigning. The full policy is published on the Country Development page.
