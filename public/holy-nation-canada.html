Who We Are

A House for People Far From Home

Holy Nation Canada began among West African families in Scarborough who wanted apostolic teaching in a country where they had arrived recently, knew almost nobody, and were working two jobs to make the first year survivable.

That origin still shapes us. This community is unusually good at receiving people who have just landed — and unusually aware that belonging somewhere matters most in the season when you belong nowhere.

“When a stranger dwells among you in your land, you shall not mistreat him. The stranger who dwells among you shall be to you as one born among you, and you shall love him as yourself.” Leviticus 19:33–34

Two languages, one house

The Sunday assembly runs in English with live French interpretation, and the Montréal city group meets primarily in French. Teaching materials from the Global College are issued in both languages. This is not a courtesy — roughly a fifth of our members are more comfortable praying in French than in English, and a house that only hears half its family is not a house.

What we are, and what we are not

  • You keep your church. Our gathering is timed to sit alongside a local Sunday service, not replace it
  • You get known. Every member is placed in a city group or a virtual fellowship
  • You get trained. The Global College ladder runs online in English and French
  • You carry something. Canada holds Watch 07 on the global roster
  • You are never asked for money to belong. Ever

Our place in the movement

Canada sits inside the TKC-North America region, currently in year three of the five-year establishment cycle. Our council is constituted and funding is transitioning to local sources, with full autonomy due in year five. We work closely with Holy Nation US, and joint gatherings across the border are a regular feature — but Canada is not a US branch, and never has been.

About the watches

Our watch on the global roster

Canada holds Watch 07 — The Watch of Government, from 12:00 to 3:00 PM Eastern. Its standing burden is rulers, courts, policy and national affairs, prayed for every day of the year.

There is a certain fitness to it. Canada is a country where public institutions still largely work, and where it is therefore easy to assume they will keep working without anyone praying for them. Holding this watch is a weekly argument against that assumption.

  • One three-hour window, one day a week — most people share a slot
  • You receive the watch guide, the current burden list and a reporting template
  • Pray alone, with your family, or in the live audio room

First Time Joining?

  • The Sunday link is posted in the community group each Saturday
  • French interpretation runs on a parallel audio channel
  • Cameras optional — nobody will put you on the spot
  • Kids are welcome; the first 20 minutes is family-friendly
  • Stay after for the open floor if you want to meet people
  • You will never be asked to give as a visitor

If You Have Just Arrived

Newcomer & Settlement Support

The first eighteen months in a new country are brutal in ways that are hard to explain to people who have not done it. Credentials do not transfer. References mean nothing. You are simultaneously overqualified and unemployable, and everyone at home assumes you are doing well.

Our compassion work in Canada is built almost entirely around that reality. It is practical, unsentimental, and offered to anyone — member or not, Christian or not.

  • Arrival practicalities — SIN, banking, phone plans, transit, school registration
  • Housing help — navigating the rental market without Canadian credit history
  • Employment — résumé conversion, interview practice, credential recognition pathways
  • Winter — a genuinely serious coat drive, because the first one catches people out
  • Isolation — a named person who checks in weekly for the first three months
  • Referral — to proper settlement agencies and legal advice, because we are not one

We do not ask about your immigration status, and we do not give immigration advice — we refer you to people licensed to give it.

How to Ask

  • Message the community group, or write to the coordinator directly
  • No form, no assessment, no waiting list for the practical stuff
  • Nothing you share goes further than the person helping you
  • You will not be asked to attend anything in return
  • If we cannot help, we will tell you who can

If you are in crisis, please also contact local emergency or crisis services. We will walk with you, and we will not pretend to be a substitute for professional help.

Questions

Frequently Asked

Do I have to leave my church to join Holy Nation Canada?

No. Most of our members belong to a local congregation with no relationship to us. The assembly is at 10:30 AM Eastern partly so it rarely clashes with a local service, and city groups meet midweek.

Is the Sunday gathering really bilingual?

Yes. Teaching is delivered in English with live French interpretation on a parallel audio channel, Global College materials are issued in both languages, and the Montréal city group operates primarily in French.

Do you help with immigration applications?

No, and we will not pretend otherwise. Immigration advice in Canada is regulated, and giving it without a licence causes real harm. We help with the practical side of arriving and refer you to licensed consultants and legal clinics for anything to do with status.

Do I have to be a member to get settlement help?

No. Help is offered on the basis of need alone, with no requirement to attend anything, believe anything or join anything. Charity with conditions attached is not charity.

Is Canada just a branch of Holy Nation US?

No. Canada has its own constituted council and its own coordinator. We sit within the TKC-North America region and work closely with the US, including annual joint gatherings, but decisions about Canada are made in Canada.

There's no city group near me. Am I stuck?

Not at all. We have members across the Prairies, Ottawa and Atlantic Canada who join the assembly online, sit in a virtual house fellowship and hold a watch from home. If you would like to start something local, three people and a willing host is the whole requirement.

Is there a membership fee?

None. Community, gatherings, watches and the Foundations Class are free. Global College tuition is set regionally with instalment plans and bursaries, and nobody has been turned away for inability to pay.
