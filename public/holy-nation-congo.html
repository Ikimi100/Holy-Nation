Who We Are

A Congolese House, Led by Congolese

The Democratic Republic of the Congo is the largest French-speaking country in the world by population, and one of the most Christian countries on earth by number of believers. It is not a place that needs foreigners to explain the Gospel to it.

Holy Nation Congo was started by Congolese believers in Kinshasa, is led entirely by Congolese, and takes its direction from them. What the wider movement supplies is teaching, training and prayer. What it does not supply is instructions.

“L'Éternel est mon berger: je ne manquerai de rien.”
“The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want.” Psaume 23:1

What we are, and what we are not

  • You keep your church. Nearly every member belongs to a Congolese congregation already
  • You get known. Every member is placed in one of the three groups
  • You get trained. Global College materials in French; sessions in French and Lingala
  • You carry something. Congo holds Watch 02 on the global roster
  • You are never asked for money to belong. Jamais

Two time zones, one country

Kinshasa runs on West Africa Time; Lubumbashi and Goma run an hour ahead on Central Africa Time. Our Sunday assembly is set by Kinshasa, which means the eastern groups join at 10h00 their time. The community group posts both every week, because assuming everyone is on the capital's clock is exactly the sort of small carelessness that makes people feel peripheral.

Our place in the movement

Congo sits in the Central Africa sub-region of TKC-Africa, in year three of the five-year cycle. Our council is forming with a Congolese chair, and full autonomy is due in year five.

Langue

Français, Lingala, Swahili

French is the language of administration, education and most written material. Lingala is the language of Kinshasa, of worship, and of how people actually speak to each other. In the east, Swahili carries much of daily life.

A house that operated only in French would be technically correct and pastorally deaf. So teaching is delivered in French, worship and prayer run in Lingala, and the Goma group works substantially in Swahili.

  • Global College materials are issued in French, translated by Congolese members where needed
  • Watch guides and prayer bulletins in French and Lingala
  • The safeguarding policy in French, Lingala and Swahili
  • Questions are answered in whichever language they are asked

Congo is one of two nations in this movement working primarily in French. We share translation work with Holy Nation Canada and the Rwandan team, so nobody rebuilds the same material twice.

Why Congo holds the Watch of Rest

Watches run in each region's local time, so several nations hold the same watch number in their own zone. Congo holds Watch 02 — The Watch of Rest, 21h00 to midnight, whose standing burden is protection over the city through the night.

We asked for this one. In much of this country a peaceful night is not something anyone takes for granted, and a watch that simply asks God for safety while people sleep is not a small or sentimental assignment here.

  • Une fenêtre de trois heures, un jour par semaine — souvent partagée
  • Se prête bien à une veille familiale avant le coucher
  • Guide de veille et liste de fardeaux fournis en français et lingala

Goma & the East

How We Speak About the East

Eastern Congo has been reported on by outsiders for decades, often carelessly. We are going to be careful.

We do not narrate this region's situation

We do not publish analysis of the security situation, name armed groups, comment on regional politics, or describe conditions in ways that could put members at risk. Our Goma group decides entirely what is said publicly about their own city — including the decision to say very little.

What we do

  • Meet, teach and pray, exactly as in Kinshasa and Lubumbashi
  • Support members displaced or under strain, quietly and without publicity
  • Refer to established humanitarian and statutory services rather than duplicating them
  • Hold the Watch of Rest over the city, every night of the year
  • Keep the group running through disruption, which is itself the point

What we do not do

  • Use the east in fundraising appeals, anywhere in the world
  • Publish names, photographs or locations without informed consent
  • Send outside visitors to observe, document or report
  • Claim to be doing more there than we are

If you want to help the east, the most useful things are prayer on the watch and giving that is not tied to a story we would have to extract from someone.

Questions

Questions Fréquentes

Dois-je quitter mon église pour vous rejoindre?

Non. Presque tous nos membres appartiennent déjà à une église congolaise, et nous découragerions quiconque de la quitter. Nous nous plaçons à côté de votre église, pas à sa place.

En quelle langue se déroulent les réunions?

L'enseignement est en français, la louange et la prière en lingala, et le groupe de Goma travaille en swahili. Vous pouvez poser vos questions dans la langue de votre choix.

Cette communauté est-elle dirigée depuis l'étranger?

Non. Elle a été fondée par des Congolais, elle est dirigée par des Congolais, et l'autonomie complète est prévue en année cinq. Le mouvement mondial fournit l'enseignement, la formation et la prière — pas des instructions.

Why do you say so little about eastern Congo?

Because our Goma members carry the risk of anything published, not us. They decide what is said about their own city, including saying very little. We also refuse to use the east in fundraising material anywhere in the world.

Y a-t-il des frais d'adhésion?

Aucun. La communauté, les assemblées, les veilles et le cours de Fondations sont gratuits. Les frais du Collège Global sont fixés régionalement avec paiement échelonné et bourses; personne n'a jamais été refusé faute de moyens.

Êtes-vous affiliés politiquement?

Non. Holy Nation Global ne soutient aucun parti ni aucun candidat, dans aucun pays, et nos groupes n'autorisent aucune campagne. La politique complète est publiée sur la page Country Development.

Faut-il être chrétien pour recevoir de l'aide?

Non. L'aide est accordée sur la base du besoin uniquement. Personne n'est interrogé sur sa foi, tenu d'assister à quoi que ce soit, ou traité différemment s'il refuse.
