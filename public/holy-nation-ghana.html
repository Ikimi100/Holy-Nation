Who We Are

A House Where Work Is Not Beneath Anyone

Ghana has an old and honourable apprenticeship culture — the mason, the seamstress, the mechanic, the carpenter, each trained under a master over years. It also has a newer and less honourable habit: treating that path as what you settle for when university does not work out.

Holy Nation Ghana was founded partly as an argument against that second habit. The first commission given to humanity was to work the ground. A man who builds well and honestly is not a lesser citizen of the Kingdom than a man who lectures about it.

“Do you see a man who excels in his work? He will stand before kings; he will not stand before unknown men.” Proverbs 22:29

What that looks like in practice

Our CIEL work here is the most developed in the movement: nine trade workshops, a structured apprenticeship pathway, savings groups, and enterprise clinics run with KBLN members who employ people themselves. It is not a charity programme bolted onto a church. It is a core part of what this house is for.

What we are, and what we are not

  • You keep your church. Most of our members belong to a Ghanaian congregation already
  • You get known. Every member is placed in a city group or a workshop
  • You get trained. Global College cohorts run on Ghanaian evenings
  • You carry something. Ghana holds Watch 01 on the global roster
  • You are never asked for money to belong. Ever

Our place in the movement

Ghana sits in the West Africa sub-region of TKC-Africa alongside Nigeria, in year three of the five-year cycle. Our sub-regional council is forming with an indigenous chair. A Global College delivery centre for West Africa outside Lagos is planned, and Accra is a candidate.

One thing we say plainly: proximity to the base house in Lagos does not make Ghana a Nigerian province. Decisions about Ghana are made in Ghana.

Take a slot

Why Ghana holds the Evening Watch

Watch 01 runs from 6:00 to 9:00 PM, and its standing burden is families, homes and the closing of the day. Ghana took it because it is the one watch that can genuinely be kept by a whole household rather than an individual slipping away alone.

Several of our families pray it together after the evening meal, with children present and participating. That is not a compromise on seriousness — it is the point. A generation that watches its parents pray will not need to be persuaded later that prayer is normal.

  • One three-hour window, one day a week — most households share a slot
  • Works as a family altar, not only a private discipline
  • You receive the watch guide, the burden list and a reporting template

CIEL Ghana

Trades, Apprenticeship & Enterprise

Nine trade workshops across Accra, Kumasi and Takoradi, each run by a master craftsman who is a member of this house, taking apprentices on a structured pathway with a written agreement.

The old apprenticeship system worked. What it lacked was written terms, a defined end point, and any protection for the apprentice. We kept the first and added the rest.

A Written Agreement

Duration, what will be taught, what is paid and when it ends — signed by both parties

The Trades

Carpentry, masonry, tailoring, welding, auto-mechanics, electrical, catering, hairdressing, phone repair

Digital Skills

A tenth workshop in Accra teaching design, bookkeeping software and online selling

Setting Up on Your Own

Tools grant, business registration help and a first-year mentor when an apprentice graduates

Savings Groups

Member-run susu circles with written rules, building capital before it is urgently needed

About CIEL

How We Protect Apprentices

Apprenticeship goes wrong in predictable ways: the term that quietly never ends, the skill that is never quite fully taught, the young person used as free labour for years. Every rule below exists because those things happen.

  • A fixed end date in writing, agreed before anyone starts
  • A syllabus — what will be taught, in what order, and how completion is assessed
  • An independent check-in every quarter by someone other than the master
  • No school-age child taken on in place of attending school
  • Safeguarding clearance for every master taking anyone under 18
  • A route to complain that does not go through the person complained about

A master who will not accept those terms does not run a workshop under this name. We have declined people over it, including people we liked.

Applying for a Place

  • Open to anyone — member or not, Christian or not
  • No fee to apply, and no fee to the apprentice
  • Placement is by aptitude and need, not by connection
  • Under-18s need a parent or guardian to countersign
  • School must come first for anyone of school age

Compassion in Ghana

What We Actually Run

Help is offered on the basis of need alone — no faith test, no attendance requirement, and a planned exit in every case.

CES — School Support

Fees, uniforms and books paid directly to schools, with attendance reviewed each term and a route into a trade at school-leaving age.

AFER — Feeding

Community kitchens in Accra and Kumasi, run by local volunteers buying from local suppliers.

CVSW — Visitation

Regular visits to widows, seniors and the chronically ill by trained, paired volunteers — the same faces each time.

Graduation from Aid

The deliberate path from relief to a trade to an enterprise. Success is a household that no longer needs the programme.

Safeguarding on every site

Every worker and every workshop master with access to children is screened, trained annually and never alone with a minor. Concerns are escalated the same day to the safeguarding lead and to statutory services where the threshold is met — including concerns about our own people.

Questions

Frequently Asked

Do I have to leave my church to join Holy Nation Ghana?

No. The 8:30 AM start exists precisely so you can attend our assembly and still make your own church service afterwards. Most of our members belong to a Ghanaian congregation already, and we would discourage anyone from leaving.

Does an apprenticeship place cost anything?

No. There is no application fee and no fee to the apprentice. Placement is by aptitude and need, not by who you know. Under-18s need a parent or guardian to countersign, and anyone of school age must remain in school.

I already run a workshop. Can I take apprentices through you?

Possibly. You would need to accept written terms with a fixed end date, a taught syllabus, quarterly independent check-ins, and safeguarding clearance if taking anyone under 18. We have declined masters who would not accept those terms, including people we liked.

Do I have to be a Christian to get a place or receive help?

No. Eligibility is based on need and aptitude alone. Nobody is asked about their faith, required to attend anything, or treated differently for declining. Charity with conditions attached is not charity.

Is Ghana run from Nigeria?

No. Ghana has its own council with a Ghanaian chair. We sit in the West Africa sub-region alongside Nigeria and share a calendar and curriculum, but decisions about Ghana are made in Ghana — and full autonomy is due in year five.

Is there a membership fee?

None. Community, gatherings, watches and the Foundations Class are free. Global College tuition is set regionally with instalment plans and bursaries, and nobody has been turned away for inability to pay.

Are you politically affiliated?

No. Holy Nation Global endorses no party and no candidate in any nation, and our groups do not permit campaigning. The full policy is published on the Country Development page and applies here without exception.
