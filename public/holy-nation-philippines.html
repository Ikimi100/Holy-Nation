Who We Are

A House Stretched Across the World

Holy Nation Philippines has three city groups — and members in nine countries. That is not unusual here. It is simply what a Filipino community looks like.

Millions of Filipinos work overseas, and the money they send home holds up households, schooling and small businesses across this country. What is discussed less often is the cost: marriages conducted over video calls, children raised by grandparents, and a parent who returns after six years to a teenager they no longer know.

“God sets the solitary in families.” Psalm 68:6

What that means for how we are built

This community was designed from the start to work across time zones, because a large share of our members were never going to be in the room. A wife in Cebu and a husband in Riyadh belong to the same house here, sit in the same city group thread, and are prayed for by name together.

  • You keep your church. Most members belong to a Filipino congregation already
  • You get known. Members abroad are placed in their home city group, not sidelined
  • You get trained. Global College cohorts run at hours that work for the Gulf and Asia
  • You carry something. The Philippines holds Watch 01 on the global roster
  • You are never asked for money to belong. Ever

Our place in the movement

The Philippines sits within TKC-Asia, which is our earliest-stage region — year two, seeding. Unlike much of that region, our community here operates openly, and this page exists because Filipino members chose that it should.

Our Main Work

Overseas Workers & the Families at Home

Two groups of people, one situation, usually supported by nobody as a single unit. We try to hold both ends.

If You Are Working Abroad

You stay in your home city group, in the same thread as your family. You are not moved to a generic "overseas" list, and you are not treated as a former member who left.

If You Are the Family at Home

Spouses raising children largely alone, and grandparents raising grandchildren, carry a load that is rarely named. The city groups are built around supporting exactly that.

What we actually do

  • Time-shifted everything — the assembly is recorded and reposted for Gulf, European and North American hours
  • Paired households — a family at home is paired with another family, so nobody is carrying a hard week alone
  • Marriage support across distance — mentoring for couples separated by work, run with the AWF marriage track
  • Parenting from far away — practical help for parents trying to stay a real presence in a child's life by phone
  • Reintegration — the return home is often harder than the leaving; we prepare families for it deliberately
  • Connection abroad — where a member lands in a country with a Holy Nation community, we introduce them personally

That last one is quietly one of the most useful things this global movement does. A Filipino nurse arriving in Toronto, Manchester or Sydney can be met by name in the first week rather than the first year.

On recruitment and employment scams

Nobody from Holy Nation will ever offer you a job abroad, charge a placement fee, arrange a visa, or ask for money or documents. We are not a recruitment agency and never will be. If anyone contacts you claiming otherwise in our name, it is a scam — please report it to theholynationglobal@gmail.com.

Why the Philippines holds the Evening Watch

Watches run in each region's local time, so several nations hold the same watch number in their own zone. The Philippines holds Watch 01 — The Evening Watch, 6:00 to 9:00 PM, whose standing burden is families, homes and the closing of the day.

We asked for this one, and the reason is not subtle. Filipino families are split across time zones more than almost any other nation's, and evening is the hour when the calls home happen. Praying for families at exactly the moment families are trying to reach each other seemed like the right assignment for this house.

  • One three-hour window, one day a week — often prayed by a whole household
  • Overseas members hold the same watch in their own local evening
  • You receive the watch guide, the burden list and a reporting template

Questions

Frequently Asked

Do I have to leave my church to join?

No. Most of our members belong to a Filipino congregation already, and we would discourage anyone from leaving. City groups meet on Friday evenings and the training strengthens you wherever you serve.

I work overseas. Can I really stay part of this?

Yes — the community was built for it. You stay in your home city group with your family, the assembly is reposted at hours that work for you, and the College cohort is timed so Gulf-based members can join live after work.

Do you help people find work abroad?

No, and we never will. We are not a recruitment agency, we do not arrange visas, and we will never charge a fee or ask for documents. Anyone contacting you with such an offer in our name is running a scam — please report it.

My spouse works abroad. Is there support for me?

Yes, and it is one of the main things we do. Households are paired so nobody carries a hard week alone, and the AWF marriage track runs mentoring specifically for couples separated by work. It is confidential.

Will I be introduced to people if I move to another country?

Where Holy Nation has a community — Canada, the UK, Australia, the US and others — yes, personally and by name, usually within your first week. Where we do not, we will be honest that we cannot.

Is there a membership fee?

None. Community, gatherings, watches and the Foundations Class are free. Global College tuition is set regionally with instalment plans and bursaries, and nobody has been turned away for inability to pay.

Are you politically affiliated?

No. Holy Nation Global endorses no party and no candidate in any nation, and our groups do not permit campaigning. The full policy is published on the Country Development page.
