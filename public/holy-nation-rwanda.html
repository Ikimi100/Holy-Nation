Who We Are

Small, Careful, and Rwandan-Led

Holy Nation Rwanda is one of our smaller communities and, by deliberate choice, one of our slowest-growing. Rwanda is a country where trust was catastrophically broken within living memory and has been rebuilt through decades of sustained national effort. Anyone arriving here with a hurry and a programme has misread the room.

So this community was started by Rwandans, is led entirely by Rwandans, and moves at the pace they set. Our role from outside is to supply teaching, training and prayer — not direction.

“Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity!” Psalm 133:1

What we are, and what we are not

  • You keep your church. Most of our members belong to a Rwandan congregation already
  • You get known. Every member is placed in a city group
  • You get trained. A regional cohort in Kigali, served from Nairobi
  • You carry something. Rwanda holds Watch 02 on the global roster
  • You are never asked for money to belong. Ever

Our place in the movement

Rwanda sits in the East Africa sub-region of TKC-Africa, in year three of the five-year cycle, served by the East Africa Training Centre in Nairobi. Our council is forming with a Rwandan chair and full autonomy is due in year five — after which the community governs itself entirely, with the standing right to disagree with any other region including the founding one.

How We Work in Rwanda

Foreign organisations have not always behaved well here. We would rather state our limits in public than be asked about them later.

We follow Rwandan leadership and Rwandan law

Reconciliation and unity work in this country is led by Rwandans through national institutions and long-established local processes. We do not run parallel programmes, we do not convene our own truth or reconciliation processes here, and we take our direction from local leaders and the national framework.

What we do

  • Teach Scripture, train leaders and pray — the same as in every other nation
  • Serve alongside existing local churches rather than competing with them
  • Take part in Umuganda community service alongside our neighbours
  • Support members practically through the compassion arms where there is need
  • Register properly, pay what is owed, and operate within the law
  • Let Rwandan members decide what is published about their own community

What we do not do

  • Speak publicly on Rwandan history, politics or national policy
  • Convene reconciliation processes of our own design
  • Use this country's past in fundraising material, anywhere in the world
  • Publish names, photographs or stories without informed, freely given consent
  • Bring in outside personnel to lead what Rwandans are already leading
  • Host visitor trips whose real purpose is the visitor's experience
“If it is possible, as much as depends on you, live peaceably with all men.” Romans 12:18
Ururimi · Language

Three Languages, One House

Rwanda is genuinely trilingual, and a community that only functions in one language quietly excludes people while insisting it welcomes everyone. So we run in all three, properly rather than tokenistically.

  • Kinyarwanda leads worship and prayer. It is the language most members think and grieve and rejoice in
  • English carries most teaching, since Global College materials arrive in it first
  • French is used in Huye and among older members, with materials issued in translation

Teaching is delivered with live interpretation, and questions are answered in whatever language they are asked in. Our facilitators are trained specifically to work across all three rather than defaulting to the one they find easiest.

This costs us time in every meeting. We consider it non-negotiable.

Materials Available In

  • Foundations Class — all three languages
  • Christ Institute — English and French; Kinyarwanda in progress
  • Kingdom Institute — English and French
  • Watch guides and prayer bulletins — all three
  • Safeguarding policy — all three

Translation into Kinyarwanda is done by Rwandan members, not by software. It is slower and it is worth it.

Why Rwanda holds the Watch of Rest

Watch 02 runs from 9:00 PM to midnight, and its standing burden is protection over the city through the night. It is the gentlest watch on the roster and it was chosen on purpose.

A community that has learned what it costs when safety disappears does not treat ordinary, uneventful nights as unremarkable. Praying for a quiet night over a quiet city is not a small assignment here.

  • One three-hour window, one day a week — most people share a slot
  • Works well as a household watch, prayed together before sleep
  • You receive the watch guide in your language, plus the burden list

Questions

Frequently Asked

Do I have to leave my church to join Holy Nation Rwanda?

No. Most of our members belong to a Rwandan congregation already, and we would discourage anyone from leaving. City groups meet on Friday evenings and the training is designed to strengthen you wherever you serve.

Which language will the meeting be in?

All three. Worship and prayer lead in Kinyarwanda, most teaching is in English with live interpretation, and Huye operates primarily in French. You may ask a question in whichever language you prefer and it will be answered in that language.

Do you run reconciliation programmes here?

No. Reconciliation and unity work in Rwanda is led by Rwandans through national institutions and established local processes. We do not run parallel programmes or convene processes of our own design. Our members participate as ordinary citizens, under local leadership.

Why is this community so small?

By choice. Rwanda has good reason to be careful about foreign organisations arriving with programmes and urgency. We would rather build something modest that lasts and is genuinely Rwandan-led than something large that is neither.

Can visitors come and serve?

With notice, and under local leadership. We do not host trips whose real purpose is the visitor's experience, and we do not permit photography of identifiable people without informed, freely given consent. Rwandan members decide what is published about their own community.

Is the award from the Kigali cohort the same as from Lagos?

Identical. Same curriculum, same examinations, same assessment standards, same register, same award. The Kigali cohort is served by the East Africa Training Centre in Nairobi and led by a licensed Rwandan facilitator.

Is there a membership fee?

None. Community, gatherings, watches and the Foundations Class are free. Global College tuition is set regionally with instalment plans and bursaries, and nobody has been turned away for inability to pay.
