Who We Are

A House in the Continent's Crossroads

South Africa is where the rest of Africa comes to work. Our Johannesburg gathering on any given Sunday holds South Africans alongside Zimbabweans, Malawians, Mozambicans, Nigerians, Congolese, Zambians and more — not as guests, but as members.

That is an ordinary fact of life here, and it is also a responsibility. A house made of many nationalities in a country where that mix has sometimes turned violent cannot be vague about what it believes on the subject.

“There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is neither male nor female; for you are all one in Christ Jesus.” Galatians 3:28

What we are, and what we are not

  • You keep your church. Most of our members belong to a South African congregation already
  • You get known. Every member is placed in a city group
  • You get trained. Global College cohorts run on South African evenings
  • You carry something. South Africa holds Watch 06 on the global roster, in SAST
  • You are never asked for money to belong. Ever

On languages

Teaching is in English, which is what the whole room shares. Worship and prayer move freely between isiZulu, Sesotho, Afrikaans, Shona and whatever else is in the room that morning. Nobody is corrected for praying in their own language.

Our place in the movement

South Africa leads the Southern Africa sub-region of TKC-Africa alongside Zambia, in year three of the five-year cycle. Our council is forming with a South African chair, and full autonomy is due in year five.

Where We Stand

A House of Many Nations

South Africa has seen periods of violence directed at people born elsewhere on this continent. A church whose own members are on both sides of that has to say something clear.

Our position is not complicated

Every person in this house belongs equally, regardless of where they were born or what documents they hold. We do not ask about immigration status, we do not rank members by nationality, and we will not tolerate contempt for the foreigner in any group, meeting or message thread we run.

What we understand

We are not naive about why tension exists. Unemployment here is severe, competition for scarce work is real, and communities under sustained economic pressure look for someone to blame. Pretending those pressures are imaginary helps nobody.

But the honest response to scarcity is to make more — more skills, more enterprise, more employment — not to drive out the neighbour. That is why our CIEL and KBLN work here is not a side project. It is the practical form of the same conviction.

  • Skills training and enterprise support offered to South Africans and migrants alike
  • KBLN members who employ people are asked to hire on merit and pay properly
  • Practical help for members facing documentation problems — through licensed advisers, not us
  • Immediate pastoral response when any member is threatened or displaced

If You Are Under Threat

  • Contact your city group host or the coordinator directly, at any hour
  • We will help with immediate safety, shelter and transport
  • We do not ask about your papers before helping you
  • We refer to licensed legal advisers, never give legal advice ourselves
  • Nothing you tell us goes further than the person helping you

If you are in immediate danger, please contact emergency services first. We will come alongside you, and we will not pretend to be a substitute for them.

Why South Africa holds the Marketplace Watch

Watches run in each region's local time, which means several nations hold the same watch number in their own zone — that is precisely how the global altar stays unbroken. South Africa holds Watch 06 — The Marketplace Watch, 9:00 AM to noon SAST.

Its standing burden is work, commerce, enterprise and the economy. In a country where unemployment is among the highest in the world, praying for work is not an abstraction. Several of our intercessors pray it from their desks between tasks.

  • One three-hour window, one day a week — most people share a slot
  • Works well for anyone employed or working from home
  • You receive the watch guide, the burden list and a reporting template

KBLN & CIEL

Work, Skills & Enterprise

South Africa has one of the highest unemployment rates in the world, concentrated brutally among the young. Any house here that only preaches and never addresses work has decided to ignore the largest single fact about the people in front of it.

So this is a substantial part of what we do, run jointly by KBLN members who employ people and CIEL trainers who prepare people to be employed — or to employ themselves.

Three KBLN Roundtables

Two in Johannesburg, one in Cape Town — founders and executives working real decisions

Work Readiness

CV writing, interview practice, and the unglamorous mechanics of actually getting hired

Skills & Trades

Practical training with local providers, plus digital skills for remote and freelance work

Enterprise Clinics

Registration, pricing, tax and hiring for members starting something of their own

Hiring Ethics

KBLN members commit to hiring on merit, paying properly and treating documentation lawfully

Questions

Frequently Asked

Do I have to leave my church to join Holy Nation South Africa?

No. Most of our members belong to a South African congregation already, and we would discourage anyone from leaving. City groups meet midweek and the training is designed to strengthen you wherever you serve.

I'm not South African. Am I welcome?

Fully and without qualification. Members of this house come from around a dozen nationalities. We do not ask about immigration status, we do not rank members by nationality, and contempt for the foreigner gets a person removed from our groups.

Do you help with documentation or immigration matters?

We help you reach licensed advisers, and we will sit with you through the process. We do not give immigration advice ourselves — doing so unlicensed causes real harm, and we have seen it happen.

Is the work readiness programme only for members?

No. Work readiness, skills training and enterprise clinics are open to anyone who needs them, on the basis of need alone, with no faith test and no requirement to attend anything.

Why isn't Country Development running here?

Because South Africa already has mature civil society, legal and reconciliation institutions built over decades. Importing a framework designed for a different context would be arrogant and unhelpful. Where our members work in those spheres, they do it through existing South African institutions.

Are you politically affiliated?

No. Holy Nation Global endorses no party and no candidate in any nation, and our groups do not permit campaigning. The full policy is published on the Country Development page and applies here without exception.

Is there a membership fee?

None. Community, gatherings, watches and the Foundations Class are free. Global College tuition is set regionally with instalment plans and bursaries, and nobody has been turned away for inability to pay.
