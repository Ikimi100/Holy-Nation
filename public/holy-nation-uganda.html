Who We Are

A House Betting on the Young

Uganda has one of the youngest populations on earth. The median age is under twenty. That single fact should reorganise how any serious church here spends its time and money — and for most it does not.

Holy Nation Uganda is built the other way round. Our teen and student work is not a department that reports to the real ministry; it is a substantial share of what we actually do, staffed by our best people rather than whoever was available.

“Let no one despise your youth, but be an example to the believers in word, in conduct, in love, in spirit, in faith, in purity.” 1 Timothy 4:12

Two languages, one house

The Sunday assembly runs in English with Luganda through worship and prayer. In Gulu, sessions run substantially in Acholi, and in Mbarara in Runyankole. We do not ask people to pray in a second language when a first one is available.

What we are, and what we are not

  • You keep your church. Most of our members belong to a Ugandan congregation already
  • You get known. Every member is placed in a city group, teen chapter or campus fellowship
  • You get trained. A regional cohort of the East Africa Training Centre runs in Kampala
  • You carry something. Uganda holds Watch 03 on the global roster
  • You are never asked for money to belong. Ever

Our place in the movement

Uganda sits in the East Africa sub-region of TKC-Africa, in year three of the five-year cycle, served by the training centre in Nairobi. Our council is forming with a Ugandan chair, and full autonomy is due in year five. Several of our members now facilitate cohorts themselves.

Take a slot

Why Uganda holds the Breaking Watch

Watch 03 runs from midnight to 3:00 AM. Its standing burden is strongholds, deliverance and spiritual conflict, and it is the slot with the highest turnover on the whole roster — people take it, find it costly, and hand it on.

We asked for it deliberately. Uganda's recent history includes things that a nation does not simply move past by ignoring them, and a watch that faces conflict directly seemed the honest one for us to hold.

  • One three-hour window, one day a week — almost always shared between three or four people
  • New intercessors are paired with an experienced watchman for the first two months
  • Nobody is thought less of for standing down from this watch

Gulu & the North
IPSD Uganda

Reconciliation Work in the North

Northern Uganda carries the memory of two decades of armed conflict. A generation grew up displaced, and a great many families are still carrying losses that were never formally acknowledged by anyone.

Our work there is led by Acholi members, moves slowly, and is deliberately unspectacular. We are not a peace agency and we do not describe ourselves as one — we are a house whose northern members are doing patient, local work among their own people.

Community Mediation

Trained local mediators working land disputes, family conflict and inter-clan tension

Trauma Care & Referral

Trained listeners who sit with people and refer onward — never a substitute for clinical care

Reintegration Support

Practical help for families receiving members home, working alongside statutory and NGO programmes

Land & Livelihood

Because most local conflict here has material causes, addressed with CIEL skills and enterprise work

Education Support

CES fees and materials for children whose schooling was interrupted or never began

About IPSD

How we talk about the north

We do not use the suffering of northern Uganda in fundraising appeals, and we do not publish photographs or stories of identifiable people without their informed, freely given consent. Visitors are welcome to serve; visitors are not welcome to document. If you are here to take pictures for a newsletter, please go somewhere else.

TANI & SANI Uganda

Four Teen Chapters, Three Campus Fellowships

In a country where most people are under twenty, treating youth work as a warm-up act is a strategic error as much as a spiritual one. Our teen chapters are led by trained adults and increasingly by older teens themselves, and they teach real doctrine rather than diluted encouragement.

  • Four teen chapters — Kampala (two), Gulu and Mbarara, meeting fortnightly
  • Three campus fellowships — student-led with a licensed adult adviser
  • Foundations taught properly — who Christ is, who they are, and how to think
  • Unfiltered question time at every meeting, with nothing ruled out of bounds
  • Annual leadership camp where teens run the sessions and deliver a service project
  • A deliberate bridge at 19 from TANI into SANI, so nobody falls through the gap

Several of our teen chapter leaders came through the chapters themselves. That is the whole design working.

Safeguarding, Without Exception

  • Every adult is screened, referenced and trained annually
  • Two cleared adults present at all times — never one alone with a minor
  • Meetings happen in visible, public-facing spaces
  • No private messaging between an adult and a minor, ever
  • Parents receive the schedule and can attend any session
  • Any concern is escalated the same day, including about our own leaders

A parent or guardian should make first contact for anyone under 18. Our full safeguarding policy is available on request.

Questions

Frequently Asked

Do I have to leave my church to join Holy Nation Uganda?

No. Most of our members belong to a Ugandan congregation already, and we would discourage anyone from leaving. City groups meet midweek and the training is designed to strengthen you wherever you serve.

Where do I study if I'm in Uganda?

A regional cohort runs in Kampala, hosted by an affiliate church and led by a licensed facilitator, served by the East Africa Training Centre in Nairobi. Same curriculum, same examinations, same award. A second cohort is planned for Gulu.

Can my teenager join?

Yes, through TANI. A parent or guardian should make first contact. Every adult is screened and trained annually, two cleared adults are present at all times, meetings happen in visible spaces, and there is no private messaging between adults and minors.

Can I visit the work in the north?

You are welcome to serve, with notice and under local leadership. You are not welcome to document. We do not host trips where the primary purpose is the visitor's experience, and we do not permit photography of identifiable people without informed consent.

Do I have to be a Christian to receive help?

No. Eligibility is based on need alone. Nobody is asked about their faith, required to attend anything, or treated differently for declining. Charity with conditions attached is not charity.

Are you politically affiliated?

No. Holy Nation Global endorses no party and no candidate in any nation, and our groups do not permit campaigning. The full policy is published on the Country Development page and applies here without exception.

Is there a membership fee?

None. Community, gatherings, watches and the Foundations Class are free. Global College tuition is set regionally with instalment plans and bursaries, and nobody has been turned away for inability to pay.
