Who We Are

An Apostolic House in a Post-Christian Country

Britain is unusual ground. It sent missionaries to most of the world for two centuries and now has emptier churches than almost anywhere on earth — while simultaneously hosting some of the most vigorous congregations in Europe, most of them African, Caribbean, Eastern European or Asian.

Holy Nation UK sits in that strange gap. We began among Nigerian families in south London, and we have grown to include people who have never set foot outside the UK. Both halves need each other, and neither is the guest.

“Do not say, ‘There are still four months and then comes the harvest’? Behold, I say to you, lift up your eyes and look at the fields, for they are already white for harvest!” John 4:35

Two things we refuse to do

We do not treat Britain as a mission field to be pitied. Condescension has never converted anyone, and a movement that arrives announcing the decline of its host will be listened to by nobody. We came to belong here, not to comment on the place.

We do not run a nostalgia society. It would be easy to become a Nigerian cultural club with a service attached — comfortable, and useless to the second generation who are British and know it. Our teaching, worship and leadership are deliberately built for people who will spend their whole lives in this country.

What we are, and what we are not

  • You keep your church. Our gathering is timed to sit alongside a local Sunday service
  • You get known. Every member is placed in a city group or a virtual fellowship
  • You get trained. The Global College ladder runs online on UK evenings
  • You carry something. The UK holds Watch 08 on the global roster
  • You are never asked for money to belong. Ever

A Word About Europe

We Are a Community, Not Yet a Region

This distinction matters, and most organisations would quietly blur it. We would rather be exact.

Europe is listed as "under prayer" in the ThyKingdomCome roadmap

That means no staff, no budget, no timeline and no constituted regional council for the continent. Holy Nation UK is a national community that functions as the liaison point — it is not a European headquarters, and we do not speak for believers in countries we have never been invited into.

What the liaison office actually does

  • Answers enquiries from anywhere in Europe and points people to what exists
  • Hosts a European virtual house fellowship on Sunday evenings, in English
  • Runs Global College cohorts on European time zones
  • Keeps a register of individual members scattered across the continent
  • Holds the correspondence if and when an invitation arrives from another nation

There are members in Ireland, the Netherlands, Germany, France, Spain and Italy. They are individuals and small groups, not national communities, and we describe them that way on purpose.

If You Are Elsewhere in Europe

  • Join the European virtual fellowship — Sundays, 6:00 PM CET
  • Enrol in the Global College on a European cohort
  • Take a watch on the global roster from wherever you are
  • If there are three of you, we will help you start something
  • An invitation from your country is how a region actually begins

About the watches

Our watch on the global roster

The UK holds Watch 08 — The Harvest Watch, from 3:00 to 6:00 PM London time. Its standing burden is souls, mission teams and open doors for the Gospel.

Given where we are praying from, it is a watch with a certain edge to it. Britain is one of the hardest fields in the world for the Gospel and one of the most consequential, and the people holding this watch are mostly praying for their own neighbours, colleagues and children.

  • One three-hour window, one day a week — most people share a slot
  • Works well as a lunchtime-to-afternoon watch for anyone working from home
  • You receive the watch guide, the burden list and a reporting template

First Time Joining?

  • The Sunday link is posted in the community group each Saturday
  • Cameras optional — nobody will put you on the spot
  • Come as you are; there is no dress code
  • Kids are welcome; the first 20 minutes is family-friendly
  • Stay after for the open floor if you want to meet people
  • You will never be asked to give as a visitor

Questions

Frequently Asked

Do I have to leave my church to join Holy Nation UK?

No. Most of our members belong to a local congregation with no relationship to us. The assembly is at 11:00 AM partly so it rarely clashes with a local service, and city groups meet midweek.

Is this an African church for African people?

It began among Nigerian families in south London and that heritage is real and honoured. It is not a requirement. A growing share of our members have no connection to Nigeria at all, and our teaching and leadership are deliberately built for people who will spend their whole lives in Britain.

Is the UK a ThyKingdomCome region?

No, and we are careful about this. Europe is listed as "under prayer" in the roadmap, which means no staff, no budget and no constituted council. The UK is a national community that serves as liaison for the continent — not a European headquarters.

I live in France, Germany or Ireland. What is there for me?

The European virtual fellowship on Sunday evenings, Global College cohorts on European time zones, and a watch on the global roster. If there are three of you in one place, write to us — that is genuinely how every national community started.

What safeguarding applies here?

Everyone working with children or vulnerable adults holds current DBS clearance and annual safeguarding training. The two-adult rule applies in every setting, and concerns are escalated to the safeguarding lead the same day and to statutory services where the threshold is met.

Is there a membership fee?

None. Community, gatherings, watches and the Foundations Class are all free. Global College tuition is set regionally with instalment plans and bursaries, and nobody has been turned away for inability to pay.

Are you politically affiliated?

No. Holy Nation Global endorses no party and no candidate in any nation, and community groups do not permit campaigning or party politics. The full policy is published on the Country Development page and applies here without exception.
