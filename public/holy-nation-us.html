Who We Are

An Apostolic House in American Soil

Holy Nation US began the way most of our national communities began — a handful of people in Houston who wanted apostolic teaching, honest accountability and somewhere to belong that wasn't built around a personality.

Three years on we are six city groups, a weekly national gathering, a Global College cohort, two prayer watches on the global roster and a set of KBLN roundtables for people working in the marketplace. Still small. Deliberately unhurried.

“Now therefore, you are no longer strangers and foreigners, but fellow citizens with the saints and members of the household of God.” Ephesians 2:19

What we are, and what we are not

We are a community, a training pipeline and a prayer altar. We are not trying to be your church, and we are not building a mega-anything. Most people here belong to a local congregation on Sunday morning and gather with us around that.

  • You keep your church. We sit alongside it — the national gathering is timed so it rarely clashes
  • You get known. Every member is placed in a city group and connected to a real person
  • You get trained. The full Global College ladder runs online from here
  • You carry something. Every city group holds a watch and a local service assignment
  • You are never asked for money to belong. Ever

Our relationship to the global house

Holy Nation US is an establishing region in year three of the five-year ThyKingdomCome cycle. That means our council is constituted, funding is transitioning to local sources, and full autonomy is due in year five — after which we govern ourselves, license our own ministers and relate to Lagos as peers rather than as a branch.

One thing worth saying plainly, because North America usually gets it the other way round: our giving does not buy us extra votes. This region has one voice in the commonwealth, exactly like Rwanda and exactly like the Philippines.

About the watches

Our watches on the global roster

Holy Nation US holds two of the eight daily watches for the whole movement. When it is mid-morning here, we are covering the hours when much of Africa and Asia is asleep — which is exactly the point of a global roster.

  • Watch 05 — The Morning Altar (6:00–9:00 AM CST): the house, its leaders, the day's assignments
  • Watch 06 — The Marketplace Watch (9:00 AM–12:00 PM CST): work, commerce, enterprise and the economy

Taking a watch means one three-hour window, one day a week. Most people share a slot with two or three others. It is the single most useful thing a new member can do.

Questions

Frequently Asked

Do I have to leave my church to join Holy Nation US?

No. Most of our members belong to a local congregation with no relationship to us at all. The national assembly is timed at 10:00 AM Central partly so that it rarely clashes with a local Sunday service, and city groups meet midweek.

Is there a membership fee or a giving requirement?

None. Joining the community, attending gatherings, taking a watch and doing the Foundations Class are all free. Global College tuition is set regionally with instalment plans and bursaries, and no one has been turned away for inability to pay.

There's no city group near me. Am I stuck?

Not at all — roughly a third of US members are the only person in their area. You join the Sunday assembly online, sit in a virtual house fellowship, and hold a watch from home. If you would like to start something local, three people and a willing host is the whole requirement.

Why isn't the Country Development pillar running here?

Because Rebuild-Nigeria was designed for a specific national context and does not transfer cleanly. Rather than rebrand it and pretend, we have left it off. If a US-appropriate expression emerges, it will come from members working in those spheres here — not from a template shipped in.

Is this politically affiliated?

No. Holy Nation Global endorses no party and no candidate in any nation, and community groups do not permit campaigning or party politics. That policy is published in full on the Country Development page and applies here without exception.

What happens when the US region reaches year five?

Full autonomy. The regional council governs itself, funds itself, licenses its own ministers and relates to the other regions as a peer. It can decline requests from any region including Lagos, and it can leave the commonwealth entirely and keep everything it has built.

Can my teenager be part of this?

TANI is not yet running in the US. When it launches it will follow the global safeguarding standard: two cleared adult admins per group, no private messaging with minors, and a parent or guardian making the first contact. Until then, teens are welcome at the Sunday assembly with their family.
