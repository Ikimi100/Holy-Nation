Who We Are

A Small House Asking a Large Question

Holy Nation Zambia is one of our smallest communities — two city groups, one prayer tower, and a few dozen committed members. We are not going to inflate that.

What we do have is an unusually clear sense of purpose, and it comes from the particular position Zambia occupies. This is a country whose constitution names it a Christian nation. Church attendance is high. Public life is saturated with Christian language. And yet Zambians will tell you plainly that declaration and discipleship are not the same thing.

“These people draw near to Me with their mouth, and honour Me with their lips, but their heart is far from Me.” Matthew 15:8

What we are, and what we are not

  • You keep your church. Nearly every member here belongs to a Zambian congregation already
  • You get known. With a community this size, that is not a promise — it is unavoidable
  • You get trained. Global College cohorts run on Zambian evenings
  • You carry something. Zambia holds Watch 05 on the global roster, in CAT
  • You are never asked for money to belong. Ever

Our place in the movement

Zambia sits in the Southern Africa sub-region of TKC-Africa alongside South Africa, in year three of the five-year cycle. Our council is forming with a Zambian chair, and full autonomy is due in year five.

Why We Are Here

Beyond Declaration

A nation can be named Christian in a document and still be discipled only shallowly in practice. Zambians say this about Zambia far more freely than outsiders do — and they are right to.

The gap we work in

Nobody in this country needs to be persuaded that Christianity is respectable. It is the water everyone swims in. That is a genuine advantage, and it also creates a specific problem: when faith is universal and assumed, it very easily becomes cultural rather than personal, and inherited rather than examined.

So our work here is deliberately narrow and deep rather than broad. We are not trying to reach the unreached in a country where almost everyone already identifies as Christian. We are trying to help a smaller number of people move from assent to formation.

  • Foundations before enthusiasm — the doctrine of Christ taught in order, examined properly
  • Personal, not inherited — every member works through what they actually believe and why
  • Character under review — the quarterly review applies to everyone, without exception
  • Integrity in ordinary life — the Citizen's Charter taken personally, in workplaces and homes
  • Small and honest — we would rather be forty formed people than four hundred attending

What We Are Careful About

  • We do not comment on Zambian politics or national policy
  • We do not tell Zambians what their constitution should say
  • We do not disparage other churches or their members
  • We do not recruit from existing congregations
  • We do not claim to have found something Zambians had missed

This is a Zambian community making a Zambian critique of Zambian church culture. It is not an outside verdict, and we would not have standing to give one.

Why Zambia holds the Morning Altar

Watches run in each region's local time, so several nations hold the same watch number in their own zone — that is precisely how the global altar stays unbroken across the day. Zambia holds Watch 05 — The Morning Altar, 6:00 to 9:00 AM CAT.

Its standing burden is the house itself, its leaders and the day's assignments. For a community this size, praying for the movement's leadership every single morning is a way of being genuinely useful to a global work while remaining small.

  • One three-hour window, one day a week — most people share a slot
  • Works well before the working day begins
  • You receive the watch guide, the burden list and a reporting template

Kitwe & the Copperbelt
Kitwe

Work on the Copperbelt

The Copperbelt's fortunes rise and fall with a commodity price set on the other side of the world. Families here have lived through boom and retrenchment more than once, and both do damage.

Our Kitwe group is small and its work is correspondingly modest, but it is aimed squarely at that cycle: helping households build something that does not depend entirely on one employer or one metal.

CVSW Visitation

Questions

Frequently Asked

Are you saying Zambian Christianity isn't real?

No, and we would have no standing to say it. This is a Zambian community making a Zambian observation about Zambian church culture — one you will hear from pastors across this country. The point is not that faith here is false; it is that where faith is universal and assumed, formation matters more, not less.

Do I have to leave my church to join?

No, and we actively discourage it. Nearly every member belongs to a Zambian congregation already. The 9:00 AM start exists precisely so you can come here and still make your own service. We do not recruit from other churches.

Do you have a view on Zambia's constitutional declaration?

We do not comment on Zambian politics or constitutional matters. What a nation puts in its founding documents is for its citizens to decide. Our concern is discipleship, which is a different question entirely.

Only two city groups. Is this worth joining?

That is a fair question. What you get here is a small community where you will genuinely be known, the full Global College ladder online, a watch on a global roster, and a share in a movement across four continents. What you will not get is a crowd.

Do I have to be a Christian to receive help?

No. Eligibility is based on need alone. Nobody is asked about their faith, required to attend anything, or treated differently for declining. Charity with conditions attached is not charity.

Is there a membership fee?

None. Community, gatherings, watches and the Foundations Class are free. Global College tuition is set regionally with instalment plans and bursaries, and nobody has been turned away for inability to pay.

Are you politically affiliated?

No. Holy Nation Global endorses no party and no candidate in any nation, and our groups do not permit campaigning. The full policy is published on the Country Development page and applies here without exception.
