What This Pillar Is

A Kingdom, Not a Subculture

For much of the last century the Church responded to a hostile culture by withdrawing from it — building a parallel world of Christian versions of everything, and quietly conceding the actual institutions to whoever wanted them.

Kingdom Operations is the correction. A king who rules only over the religious sphere is not a king; he is a chaplain. If Christ is Lord of all, then His people belong in the classroom, the treasury, the studio, the laboratory, the court and the farm — not as infiltrators, but as citizens doing excellent work under a different allegiance.

“The kingdoms of this world have become the kingdoms of our Lord and of His Christ, and He shall reign forever and ever.” Revelation 11:15

How the Kingdom actually enters a sphere

Not by protest, and not by capture. Territory in a sphere is taken the same way it always has been: by people who are unusually competent, unusually honest, and present for a very long time.

  • Excellence — be undeniably good at the work itself, before claiming anything about it
  • Integrity — refuse the standard compromises even when they are the local norm
  • Presence — stay long enough to reach the level where decisions are actually made
  • Institution-building — build things that outlast you; movements die, institutions compound
  • Discipleship — bring others up behind you, or the ground is lost in one generation

The Framework

The Twelve Spheres of Influence

The twelve domains through which The Holy Nation carries the Kingdom into every sector of national life. Each has a working group, a mentor pool and an annual assignment.

01

Kingdom Church Training

Equipping the Church itself — sound doctrine, trained leadership and congregations that make disciples rather than crowds.

02

Kingdom Family & Citizenship

Marriage, parenting, the household altar and what it means to be a citizen of two kingdoms at once.

03

Kingdom Education & Culture

Curriculum, teaching and the forming of culture. Whoever forms a child's mind at seven has largely decided what they will defend at forty.

04

Kingdom Governance & Leadership

Public office, policy and civil service. Righteous governance is the ordinary duty of a citizen who fears God, not a partisan project.

05

Kingdom Economy & Business

Enterprise, capital, employment and trade. Wealth created honestly is a Kingdom instrument; wealth extracted dishonestly is theft with paperwork.

06

Kingdom Media

News, publishing and public information. Truth-telling under commercial pressure is one of the hardest disciplines in any society.

07

Kingdom Art & Entertainment

Story, music, film and design. Culture is discipled by what people love, and people love what they are given to imagine.

08

Kingdom Technology & IT

Research, engineering and innovation. Believers building the tools everyone else will use, with ethics attached from the beginning.

09

Kingdom Health & Wellbeing

Clinical care, public health and research. The sphere where compassion and competence are least separable.

10

Kingdom Social Security

Welfare, protection and provision for the vulnerable — the widow, the orphan, the stranger and those who cannot defend themselves.

11

Kingdom Prayer & Defense

Sustained intercession and spiritual defence over people, cities and nations — the altar behind every other sphere.

12

Kingdom Missions & Evangelism

Carrying the Gospel to cities, states and nations, and discipling the territories that receive it.

How a sphere working group operates

Each sphere has a working group made up of people who actually work in it — not commentators. Groups meet quarterly, carry one annual assignment, and report to the Kingdom Congress each June.

  • Map the sphere in your nation: who decides, what the pressure points are, where believers already are
  • Identify one concrete assignment for the year — a policy, an institution, a standard, a training pipeline
  • Recruit and mentor younger people into the sphere deliberately, not accidentally
  • Publish what you learn so the next nation does not start from zero

4 Age Tiers
Arm 01

Kingdom Education

A complete formation curriculum from early childhood to school-leaving age — designed so that a young person arrives at eighteen with a coherent view of God, self, work and society, rather than a collection of Sunday-school fragments.

Kingdom Education runs in three settings: inside congregations, inside partner schools, and at home with parents who have been trained and resourced to teach it themselves.

Kids — Ages 3 to 6

Story, wonder and the character of God. Formation through repetition, song and play

Low Graders — Ages 7 to 10

The whole biblical narrative in order, plus honesty, courage, work and friendship

Middle Graders — Ages 11 to 14

Doctrine, hard questions, identity and the first serious engagement with objections

High Graders — Ages 15 to 18

Worldview, vocation, ethics and the twelve spheres. Handover to TANI and SANI

Teacher & Parent Training

Certification for anyone delivering the curriculum, in a school, a church or a home

Arm 02

CIEL

The Centre for Creativity, Investment & Economic Liberty — the economic engine of this pillar, working on the unglamorous question of how ordinary people move from dependence to productive freedom.

Poverty is rarely solved by generosity alone. It is solved by skills, capital, markets, property rights and enterprises that employ people. CIEL exists to build all five, and to do it without the paternalism that has soured so much development work.

Creativity & Skills

Trade training, craft, digital skills and the recovery of work as dignity rather than punishment

Investment & Capital

Micro-capital, savings groups and a Kingdom investment pool for viable small enterprises

Economic Liberty

Advocacy on property rights, business registration and the regulatory traps that keep the poor informal

Enterprise Clinics

Practical clinics run with KBLN on structure, pricing, hiring, tax and scale

Graduation from Aid

A deliberate pathway moving compassion beneficiaries into skills, then work, then enterprise

Arm 03

The Commonwealth International

A collaboration platform across nations, professions and institutions — so that what is learned in one place is not painfully re-learned everywhere else.

Cross-Border Exchange

Professionals in the same sphere across different nations, comparing what works and what only appeared to.

The Shared Library

Case studies, curricula, model policies and templates, published openly for any group to adapt.

Skills & Placement

Short placements and secondments so practitioners can learn in a working institution, not a seminar.

Joint Ventures

Shared projects between nations — schools, clinics, enterprises — co-owned and co-funded.

Why a commonwealth, and not a headquarters

Centralised movements export one nation's assumptions to every other nation and call it strategy. The Commonwealth is deliberately built the other way round: each nation holds its own work, and the platform exists to move knowledge, people and capital between them.

Nigeria has things to teach Kenya about informal enterprise. Kenya has things to teach the United Kingdom about mobile finance. The United Kingdom has things to teach both about institutional durability. None of that transfers through a chain of command — only through relationships between practitioners who respect each other.

  • Every member nation retains full ownership of its own institutions
  • Contributions to the shared library are open, attributed and free to adapt
  • Joint ventures are co-owned, with governance agreed in writing before funding
  • No nation's model is treated as the default for everyone else

Current Focus Areas

  • Teacher certification and curriculum sharing
  • Micro-enterprise finance models
  • Anti-corruption practice in public service
  • Community health and clinic operations
  • Agricultural cooperatives and land stewardship
  • Media standards and truth-telling under pressure

Arm 04

The Kingdom Union

The convening body of this pillar. The Union brings the twelve sphere working groups into one room each year, aligns their assignments, and issues the Kingdom Agenda for the coming cycle.

It is a deliberative body, not a governing one. It cannot bind any church, business or nation — its authority is entirely the authority of a well-made argument agreed by people who are actually doing the work.

The Declaration

The Kingdom Agenda

Seven articles agreed by the Union. They are commitments, not slogans — each one carries a measurable assignment for the year.

Article I — The Lordship of Christ Over Every Sphere

We hold that there is no neutral territory in human life, and no sphere in which the authority of Christ is merely advisory. We therefore reject both the retreat that abandons institutions and the triumphalism that seeks to seize them by force.

Article II — The Primacy of the Family

We hold that the household is the first institution and the foundation of every other. We commit to strengthening marriages, equipping parents to disciple their own children, and refusing programmes that quietly substitute the church for the home.

Article III — The Formation of the Young

We hold that a generation is discipled or lost in the first eighteen years. We commit to a complete education curriculum, certified teachers, and a deliberate handover of every young person into a sphere, a mentor and a plan.

Article IV — Honest Wealth and Productive Work

We hold that work is dignity and honest enterprise is a Kingdom instrument. We commit to skills, capital and market access for the poor, and we name corruption, exploitation and extraction as theft regardless of their legality.

Article V — Righteousness in Public Office

We hold that public office is a trust and not a harvest. We commit to preparing believers for governance, supporting them once there, and declining to defend anyone in our own ranks who betrays that trust.

Article VI — Justice for the Vulnerable

We hold that a society is judged by what it does to those who cannot defend themselves. We commit to the protection of children, the care of the widow and the stranger, and the pursuit of legal remedy for those denied it.

Article VII — Institutions That Outlive Us

We hold that movements dissipate and institutions compound. We commit to building schools, clinics, enterprises and councils that will function well after their founders are gone, and to handing them over rather than holding them.

Questions

Frequently Asked

Is this a political movement?

No. We are not aligned with any party, we do not endorse candidates, and we do not campaign. Government is one of twelve spheres, and our interest in it is the same as our interest in agriculture: that believers work in it with excellence and integrity.

Isn't "taking the spheres" just Christian dominance under a new name?

It would be, if the method were seizure. It isn't. The Agenda explicitly rejects triumphalism, and every practical instruction in this pillar is about competence, honesty, service and building institutions that serve everyone — including people who share none of our beliefs.

Do I need theological training to join a working group?

No. You need to actually work in the sphere. The Kingdom Institute (Stage 02 of the Global College) is recommended because it sharpens the thinking, but a competent nurse with no formal theology is more use to the health working group than a theologian with no clinical experience.

Can non-Christians participate in CIEL or Commonwealth projects?

Yes. Skills training, enterprise finance, clinics and schools serve anyone who needs them, with no faith requirement attached to receiving help and no pressure applied as a condition of it.

Is the Kingdom Education curriculum a replacement for school?

No. It is a formation curriculum that runs alongside ordinary academic education, delivered in a congregation, a partner school's chaplaincy programme, or at home. It does not teach mathematics or replace a national syllabus.

Does the Kingdom Union have authority over my church or business?

None whatsoever. The Union is deliberative. It publishes agreed priorities and research; it cannot bind, levy, instruct or discipline any church, business or individual. Its influence is only ever the influence of a good argument.

How is progress in a sphere actually measured?

By the annual assignment, reported publicly at the Congress. A working group states one concrete objective at the start of the year — a curriculum adopted, a policy changed, a training pipeline built, a standard published — and reports honestly on whether it happened, including when it did not.
