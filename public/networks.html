What This Pillar Is

Nobody Grows Alone

A believer can sit under excellent teaching for a decade and still stall — not for lack of truth, but for lack of peers. People who carry what you carry, face what you face, and can tell you the truth because they are standing in the same place.

That is what the networks are for. Each one gathers a specific constituency, gives it a table of its own, and connects it to mentors who have already walked that road. A pastor in a village congregation, a founder running two hundred staff, a nineteen-year-old in her second year at university — all need the same doctrine, and all need very different rooms to work it out in.

“As iron sharpens iron, so a man sharpens the countenance of his friend.” Proverbs 27:17

What every network holds in common

The networks are not five different ministries with five different messages. They are one house arranged into five rooms. Whatever network you join, these things are constant.

  • One doctrine — the Global College curriculum underpins every network's teaching
  • A local chapter — you are placed in a named group that meets on a fixed rhythm
  • A mentor — every member is connected to someone further along in the same sphere
  • An annual assembly — each network convenes fully at the Global Apostolic Summit
  • A field assignment — networks are not discussion clubs; each carries real work

The Unit

How a Chapter Works

A network you can only read about is a mailing list. The chapter is where a network actually becomes real — a named group, in a named place, meeting on a fixed date.

Network Chapter type Rhythm Typical size
IFAM Regional ministers' roundtable Monthly 12 – 30
KBLN Executive roundtable Monthly 8 – 12
SANI Campus chapter Weekly in term 15 – 40
AWF Season circle Fortnightly 10 – 25
TANI Teen chapter Fortnightly 10 – 30

Chapters are hosted by local congregations, campuses or members' homes and businesses. Where no chapter exists in your area, we will help you start one — see the pathway below.

Starting a Chapter

Step 01

Express Interest

Tell us which network, which city and roughly how many people you already have. Three committed people is enough to begin a conversation.

Step 02

Leader Screening

The proposed chapter leader is interviewed, referenced and — for SANI and TANI — safeguarding cleared before anything is announced.

Step 03

Training & Materials

A two-day chapter leader training, plus the network handbook, teaching curriculum and reporting templates.

Step 04

Launch & Covering

The chapter is formally recognised, added to the directory, and assigned to a regional coordinator who checks in monthly.

What a Chapter Commits To

  • Meet on the published rhythm, without drifting
  • Teach from the network curriculum, not private material
  • File a short quarterly report to the regional coordinator
  • Send delegates to the annual network assembly
  • Carry at least one field assignment each year
  • Uphold the safeguarding policy without exception

Chapters that stop reporting are contacted, supported, and — if still inactive after two quarters — quietly retired from the directory. A directory full of chapters that do not meet helps nobody.

What affiliation is not

We are deliberate about this, because too many networks have used the language of covering to take what did not belong to them.

  • It is not a transfer of your church's property, assets or bank accounts
  • It is not a change of your church's name or legal identity
  • It is not a levy, tithe or compulsory financial obligation
  • It is not control over who you hire, ordain or appoint
  • It is not permanent — affiliation can be ended by either party in writing, without penalty

What it is: a doctrinal alignment, a peer group, an overseer with the standing to ask you hard questions, and a movement you can draw on when the work outgrows you.

Affiliation Requirements

  • Agreement with the statement of faith
  • Two references from recognised ministers
  • Completion of the Apostolic Institute, or equivalent assessed by the council
  • A safeguarding policy in place and in use
  • Willingness to submit to annual review

Questions

Frequently Asked

Do I have to leave my church to join a network?

No, and we would discourage it. The networks are designed to sit alongside your local congregation, not replace it. Most network members belong to churches with no formal relationship to Holy Nation Global at all.

Is there a membership fee?

No. Joining a network and belonging to a chapter costs nothing. Conferences and residential camps carry a delegate fee to cover venue and catering, and bursaries are available for anyone who cannot meet it.

Can I belong to more than one network?

Yes, where it genuinely fits — a woman who runs a business commonly sits in both AWF and KBLN, for example. We do ask you to name one as your primary chapter so that somebody is clearly responsible for you.

There is no chapter in my city. What now?

Join the online chapter for your network while we work with you to start a local one. You need three committed people and a leader willing to be screened and trained — that is the whole requirement.

What safeguarding applies to SANI and TANI?

Every adult working with under-18s is background checked, trained annually, and never alone with a minor. Chapters meet in visible spaces, parents receive the schedule in advance, and any concern is escalated the same day. Our safeguarding policy is public on request.

Does IFAM affiliation mean you control my church?

No. You retain your name, legal identity, property, finances and appointments. Affiliation is doctrinal alignment plus voluntary accountability, and either party can end it in writing without penalty. See "what affiliation is not" above.

Are women licensed and ordained through this movement?

Yes. Women complete the same Global College ladder, sit the same examinations and are licensed and ordained on the same terms. AWF provides additional mentoring and covering alongside that pathway.
