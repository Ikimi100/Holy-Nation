What This Pillar Is

The Altar and the Road

Two things hold this pillar together. The altar — sustained, structured, unbroken intercession over people, cities and nations. And the road — teams that physically go where the praying has been done, and stay long enough to leave something behind.

We refuse to separate them. Prayer without mission becomes a private religious hobby. Mission without prayer becomes exhausting activity that produces converts nobody disciples. Held together, they are how territories actually change.

“My house shall be called a house of prayer for all nations.” Isaiah 56:7

How this pillar is organised

Everything here operates on a simple escalation: an individual keeps a watch, watches gather into a tower, towers report into a command, and the command deploys teams to cities, states and nations. Nobody prays into a vacuum — every hour on the roster is logged, and every field report goes back to the people who prayed.

  • The Watch — a three-hour slot on the 24-hour roster, kept by an individual or a small group
  • The Tower — a city-based company of intercessors covering a defined territory
  • The Command — regional coordination that sets targets, assigns burdens and tracks answers
  • The Mission — deployed teams carrying the Gospel to cities, states and nations

The Roster

The Eight Daily Watches

Twenty-four hours divided into eight three-hour watches, each with its own scriptural character and standing burden. Take one slot, one day a week — that is all it takes to hold a piece of the wall.

Watch Hours Name Standing Burden
01 6 – 9 PM The Evening Watch Families, homes and the closing of the day
02 9 PM – 12 AM The Watch of Rest Protection over the city through the night
03 12 – 3 AM The Breaking Watch Strongholds, deliverance and spiritual conflict
04 3 – 6 AM The Dawn Watch Breakthrough, new mercies and prophetic release
05 6 – 9 AM The Morning Altar The house, its leaders and the day's assignments
06 9 AM – 12 PM The Marketplace Watch Work, commerce, enterprise and the economy
07 12 – 3 PM The Watch of Government Rulers, courts, policy and national affairs
08 3 – 6 PM The Harvest Watch Souls, mission teams and open doors for the Gospel

Watches run in each region's local time, so the global altar is genuinely unbroken across time zones. Full watch guides — scripture, prayer points and reporting template — are issued when you enrol.

How to Take a Watch

  • Choose one watch and one day of the week to begin
  • You receive the watch guide and the current burden list
  • Pray alone, with your family, or join the live audio room
  • Log your hour and note anything you sensed or saw
  • Your tower leader collates reports monthly

Start with one three-hour slot per week. Most people who try to start with more stop within a month.

Structure

Kingdom Army Command

The coordinating structure behind the towers. It exists so that intercession is not duplicated, gaps are visible, burdens are assigned deliberately, and answered prayer is actually recorded.

The Intercessor

Holds a watch. Prays the issued burden, keeps a personal log, and reports upward. The whole system rests on this one role being done faithfully.

The Watchman

Leads a watch company. Trains new intercessors, holds the roster for that slot, and files the watch report.

The Tower Leader

Carries a named city. Sets the territorial research, chairs the weekly gathering, and consolidates all eight watches into one monthly report.

Regional Command

Oversees several towers. Assigns burdens, resolves gaps, calls emergency assemblies, and recommends territories for deployment.

From Burden to Deployment

How a territory moves from being prayed about to being physically entered.

Phase 01

Research the Ground

The tower assembles a territorial profile: population, institutions, history, existing churches, social conditions, and the specific resistance the Gospel meets there.

Phase 02

Sustained Intercession

A minimum of ninety days of targeted prayer across all eight watches before any team is considered. No exceptions, however urgent the opportunity looks.

Phase 03

Reconnaissance Visit

A small team visits, meets local believers and leaders, and tests what the tower has been sensing against what is actually on the ground.

Phase 04

Deployment

A full mission team is sent under one of the three tiers, with a defined length, budget, local partner and measurable objective.

Phase 05

Handover & Continuation

Converts are joined to a local congregation, a local tower is planted, and the sending tower keeps praying for at least a year after the team leaves.

Mission

Three Tiers of Advance

Cities, states, nations. Same Gospel, escalating scale — and a different strategy at each level.

Tier 01City-wide

The Glory of Christ — To Cities

City-wide evangelistic gatherings built around one thing: the public display of the person and work of Christ. Not a personality, not a programme — Christ, preached plainly, in the open, to a whole city at once.

Each campaign runs across three to five days and is always hosted jointly with local churches, who keep every convert. We do not plant competing congregations in cities that already have a healthy church.

What a city campaign includes

  • Ninety days of tower intercession before arrival
  • Pastors' breakfast and joint planning with local churches
  • Open-air evening gatherings with preaching and ministry
  • Daytime outreach: markets, campuses, hospitals, prisons
  • Medical and compassion clinics run with the HACO team
  • Follow-up handover to named local congregations within 14 days

Tier 02State-level

Kingdom Advance — To States

Where a city campaign gathers a crowd, a state advance builds a structure. This tier targets an entire state or province and aims at something more durable than an event: a functioning network of leaders, towers and trained workers that remains long after the team goes home.

Kingdom Advance always runs in partnership with the Networks and Country Development pillars, because at state level the Gospel has to engage institutions, not just individuals.

What a state advance includes

  • A leaders' summit convening ministers from across the state
  • Planting or strengthening prayer towers in three to five cities
  • A regional cohort of the Global College launched on site
  • Engagement with civic, education and business leadership
  • Commissioning of a state coordinator and a standing council
  • Twelve-month follow-through with quarterly review visits
ScaleOne state / province
Duration7 – 10 days on site

Tier 03NationalLong-term

Thy Kingdom Come — To Nations

The highest tier, and the slowest. A national assignment is measured in years, not days. It begins with a standing national prayer altar, moves through leader development and institution-building, and aims at the discipling of a nation rather than the evangelisation of a crowd.

This tier feeds directly into the ThyKingdomCome Movement and the Country Development pillar, where the work continues in governance, education, economy and social reform.

What a national assignment includes

  • A national prayer altar with permanent local leadership
  • A national apostolic council drawn from existing local leaders
  • A Global College delivery centre for indigenous training
  • Compassion and social intervention programmes rooted locally
  • Engagement with governance, policy and national reorientation
  • Deliberate handover to national leadership within five years

What Mission Teams Actually Need

People assume mission requires a pulpit. Most of what makes a campaign work is nothing of the kind.

Medical & Care

Doctors, nurses, pharmacists and counsellors for the field clinics run alongside every city campaign.

Logistics

Transport, procurement, accommodation, catering and site management. The least visible and most decisive role.

Media & Documentation

Photography, video, live sound and reporting so that what happens is captured and the senders can see it.

Children & Youth

Dedicated teams running parallel programmes wherever a campaign gathers families.

Before You Apply

  • You must be in active membership somewhere
  • A reference from your pastor or mentor is required
  • Teams train together for six weeks before departure
  • Safeguarding clearance is mandatory for all child-facing roles
  • Travel and vaccination requirements vary by destination

No prior mission experience is needed for city campaigns. State and national assignments require at least one completed campaign.

Questions

Frequently Asked

Do I have to pray for three hours straight?

No. A watch is a three-hour window that you cover — most intercessors pray in two or three stretches within it, or share the slot with two or three others. What matters is that the window is not empty.

I've never done anything like this. Am I qualified?

Yes. The only requirement is that you know Christ and will show up. Every new intercessor receives a written watch guide with scripture, structure and prayer points, and is placed alongside an experienced watchman for the first two months.

How do you decide which cities and nations to target?

Through a combination of tower burden, invitation from local churches, and regional command assessment. We do not enter a territory uninvited, and we do not go anywhere that has not had at least ninety days of sustained prayer first.

Do you plant churches in cities you visit?

Only where there is genuinely no viable local congregation. Our default is to hand every convert to an existing local church, including churches with no relationship to us. Campaigns that harvest for themselves are a form of theft.

Is there a cost to join a mission team?

Team members usually raise their own travel costs, and the campaign budget covers accommodation, food and ground logistics. Where a team member cannot raise travel, the missions fund covers it — ask, and it is handled confidentially.

Can my church host a city campaign?

Yes. Hosting requires an existing or new prayer tower in your city, agreement among a group of local churches rather than one alone, and a twelve-month lead time. Start with the form above.

What happens to my prayer request after I send it?

It is read by a member of the intercession team, entered on the current watch list under your first name only, and prayed over daily for at least thirty days. We will follow up by email to ask how you are, unless you ask us not to.
