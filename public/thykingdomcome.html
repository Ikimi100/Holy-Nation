What This Pillar Is

One Prayer, Many Nations

The movement takes its name from the plainest line in the prayer Christ taught: Thy Kingdom come, Thy will be done, on earth as it is in heaven. It is a request with a geography. Somewhere, on actual ground, among actual people.

ThyKingdomCome is the pillar that carries the other seven across borders. Where the House gathers, the College trains, the towers pray and the compassion arms serve — TKC is the structure that makes all of it reproducible in a place none of us has ever lived.

“Your kingdom come. Your will be done on earth as it is in heaven.” Matthew 6:10

The thing we are deliberately not doing

Most international religious expansion follows a familiar pattern: a headquarters in one country, branches everywhere else, decisions made at the centre, and local leaders who remain junior for life. It produces dependence, resentment and a movement that collapses the moment the founding nation's funding does.

TKC is built the other way round. A region is established with a written handover date. Indigenous leaders are identified in year one, not year ten. Funding flows toward local self-sufficiency rather than perpetual subsidy. And when the handover happens, it is real — including the right to disagree with us afterwards.

  • Indigenous from the start — local leadership identified and trained in year one
  • A written exit — every region has a handover date agreed before it launches
  • Locally funded, locally governed — self-sufficiency is the target, not an aspiration
  • Contextual, not copied — the doctrine travels; the cultural packaging does not
  • Peers, not branches — after handover, regions relate as equals in a commonwealth

TKC-Asia

The earliest-stage region, and the one we publish least about. In several of the contexts where this work is beginning, being publicly identified with a foreign religious movement can cost a local believer their job, their family relationships or their safety.

So we do not name locations, publish photographs, or list local leaders. That is not evasion; it is the minimum duty of care owed to people who carry far more risk than we do.

How this region operates

  • Relationship-led and slow, working through existing local believers
  • No named locations, images or leader lists published anywhere
  • Training delivered privately and in local languages
  • Local leaders decide entirely what is made public, and when
  • Full compliance with local law — we do not encourage anyone to break it
  • Prayer support coordinated through the towers without naming places

TKC-North America

Established initially through African diaspora congregations, and now broadening into indigenous North American churches and networks. The region carries a distinctive tension: it holds significant resources, and resources tend to buy influence whether anyone intends it or not.

We have addressed that structurally. North American funding does not carry North American votes. The region has one voice in the commonwealth, exactly like every other.

The Cycle

How a Region Is Established

Five years, five stages, and a handover date written down before anything begins.

Year 00 · Before Anything

Prayer and Invitation

No region begins without a standing invitation from believers already in that place, and at least ninety days of tower intercession. We do not enter uninvited, and we do not respond to opportunity alone.

Year 01 · Seeding

The Altar and the First Leaders

A regional prayer altar is established, and the first indigenous leaders are identified and enrolled in the Global College. This is year one, not year five — the whole model depends on it.

Year 02 – 03 · Forming

Council and Training Capacity

A regional council is constituted with an indigenous chair. A Global College delivery centre opens so training happens locally. Networks and compassion arms begin, adapted to local conditions.

Year 04 · Transitioning

Financial and Governance Transfer

Funding shifts decisively toward local sources. Governance decisions move to the regional council. External personnel begin withdrawing on a published schedule.

Year 05 · Handover

Full Autonomy

The region governs itself, funds itself, licenses its own ministers and joins the commonwealth as a peer — with the standing right to disagree with the founding region publicly.

What Handover Actually Means

  • The regional council appoints its own leaders without approval from us
  • Local assets are held locally, in local legal entities
  • The region sets its own budget and raises its own funds
  • It licenses and ordains its own ministers on the agreed standard
  • It may decline any request from any other region, including ours
  • It may leave the commonwealth entirely, keeping everything it built

The last point is the test of whether the rest is true. A handover that cannot be walked away from is a lease.

Our Principles
Non-Negotiable

Seven Principles of Expansion

Cross-cultural mission has an ugly history alongside its honourable one. These principles exist because of the ugly half.

I — We go only where we are invited

By believers already in that place. An open door, a funding opportunity or a strategic map is not an invitation. Where there is no invitation, we pray and we wait.

II — Indigenous leadership from year one

Local leaders are identified, trained and given real authority in the first year, not held in permanent apprenticeship while foreigners make the decisions. If we cannot find local leaders, that is a reason to slow down, not to import replacements.

III — The doctrine travels; the culture does not

Christ, Scripture and the apostolic pattern are the same everywhere. Music, dress, meeting times, organisational structure, decision-making style and forms of address are local property, and we have no standing to legislate on them.

IV — We do not compete with existing churches

Where a healthy local church exists, we strengthen it rather than planting alongside it. Converts from any campaign are handed to local congregations, including ones with no relationship to us. Sheep-stealing dressed as revival is still theft.

V — Funding does not buy governance

A region that gives more money does not get more votes. Contributions are made without conditions attached to the receiving region's decisions, and any funder attempting otherwise is refused.

VI — We protect people before we publicise work

In sensitive contexts, no names, images or locations are published without the explicit consent of local leaders who understand the risk far better than we do. No report, campaign or fundraising appeal is worth a person's safety.

VII — We obey the law of the land

We register properly, pay what is owed, and do not encourage anyone to break their nation's laws. Where a law forbids what conscience requires, that is a decision for local believers to make for themselves — not for us to make on their behalf from a safe distance.

Once a year every region sends delegates to the Global Apostolic Summit, and the commonwealth meets face to face. It is the only occasion on which the whole movement is in one room.

Questions

Frequently Asked

Is TKC a denomination?

No. It is a movement of congregations, networks and institutions holding a shared doctrine and a shared mandate. Affiliate churches keep their own name, governance, property and appointments. See the Networks pillar for what affiliation does and does not mean.

What happens to a region after handover?

It becomes a peer in the commonwealth: self-governing, self-funding, licensing its own ministers, and free to decline any request from any other region. It can also leave entirely and keep everything it has built. That freedom is what makes the handover real.

Why won't you publish details about Asia?

Because in several of those contexts, public association with a foreign religious movement can cost a local believer their livelihood, their family or their safety. Local leaders decide what is published and when. No report or appeal is worth that risk.

Do you plant churches where churches already exist?

No. Where a healthy local church exists, we strengthen it. Converts from campaigns are handed to local congregations, including ones with no relationship to us. We plant only where there is genuinely no viable congregation.

Does giving more money mean more influence?

No, and this is structural rather than aspirational. Each region has one voice regardless of contribution, funding is accepted only without conditions on the receiving region's decisions, and funders who attempt otherwise are refused.

How do I start TKC in my country?

Write to us using the form above. What matters most is that there are already believers in your nation who are praying about it — a region begins with a genuine local invitation, not with a decision made in Lagos.

Why are Europe and Latin America listed but not active?

Because they are under prayer and nothing more. No staff, no budget, no timeline. We list them so nobody is misled in either direction — we are not hiding work there, and we are not pretending to have any.
